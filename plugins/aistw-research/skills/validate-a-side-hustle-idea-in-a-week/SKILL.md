# Validate a Side Hustle Idea in One Week

## When to use

Anyone with a side hustle idea who wants proof of demand before spending real money.

## Steps

1. Friday, 2 to 3 hours: map where your customers already complain and note their exact words. Map competitors too. Competition means money is being spent; zero competition often means others decided it was not worth it.
2. Name 10 specific people to contact. Friends do not count for validation: their "sounds great" is the number one failure mode.
3. Saturday, 3 to 4 hours: run 5 conversations about past behavior, not hypotheticals. Ask: how are you solving this right now, what does it cost you in time or money, what have you tried, what is the most frustrating part. Never ask "would you use this?"
4. Listen 80 percent of the time. Pitch nothing. Patterns usually emerge by interview 8 to 10 if you keep going.
5. Sunday, 2 to 3 hours: run one action test. A simple landing page with an email or early-access signup, a small deposit or pre-sale, or a manual "Wizard of Oz" version of the service delivered by hand.
6. Judge against kill criteria you set before starting: for example, if fewer than 30 percent of about 20 interviewees show strong interest, reassess or kill the idea. CB Insights data cited by validation guides puts "no market need" behind 42 percent of startup failures.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me validate a side hustle idea in one week. Plain, practical, no hype.

Idea: [one sentence]
Who I think the customer is: [be specific]
What I think they pay for now: [current alternatives]

Write:
1. A Friday desk-research checklist: where to find complaints, what competitor signals to look for, how to pick 10 people to contact.
2. A Saturday interview script: 8-10 questions about past behavior only. No hypotheticals, no pitching.
3. A Sunday action test plan: landing page outline with headline and CTA, or a pre-sale offer, or a manual concierge test.
4. Kill criteria: specific numbers that mean "reassess or kill".

Rules:
- Validation means strangers willing to pay or sign up, not compliments.
- Show me the draft first. I will review before using anything.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Asking friends and family. Polite encouragement is not demand.
- Asking hypotheticals like "would you use this?" People say yes and mean no.
- Skipping the action test. Conversations inform; only a signup, click on pricing, or payment validates.
- Moving the goalposts. Set kill criteria before you start, not after you fall in love with the idea.
