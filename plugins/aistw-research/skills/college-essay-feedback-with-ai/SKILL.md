# Get College Essay Feedback From AI Without Losing Your Voice

## When to use

Applicants who want a second set of eyes without crossing any college's AI line.

## Steps

1. Write the full draft yourself first. AI feedback only works on real writing with your voice in it.
2. Check your target schools' policies. They vary: Brown does not permit AI on application content under any circumstances, while Caltech allows grammar and spelling review but not AI drafting or outlining.
3. Ask for critique, not rewrites. Good prompt: "Point out where my transition feels abrupt." Bad prompt: "Rewrite my introduction."
4. Use the Tutor Test from Spark Admissions: would a human tutor suggest this edit while leaving the writing to you? If yes, it is feedback. If no, it is authorship.
5. Apply the Authorship Test: if you cannot explain the stylistic choice behind every sentence, you did not author it.
6. Keep dated drafts with version history as proof of authorship. There was no formal AI-disclosure section on the Common App as of the Fall 2026 cycle, so your drafts are your evidence.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Give me feedback on my college essay draft. Critique only, no rewriting.

Essay draft:
[paste your draft]

Give me:
1. Three specific strengths, with the exact lines.
2. Three specific weaknesses: where the logic jumps, where the voice flattens, where a transition fails.
3. Five questions a reader would still have after finishing.
4. One paragraph on whether the opening earns the reader's attention.

Rules:
- Do NOT rewrite any sentence. Do NOT suggest replacement wording.
- Point at problems; let me fix them.
- Flag anything that sounds generic enough that a thousand applicants could have written it.
- Show me the feedback. I will do all revisions myself.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Sentence-level rewrites and tone upgrades sit in the yellow zone: they can erase your voice and cross policies.
- Fabricating or polishing anecdotes with AI. Admissions officers say AI-written essays lack authenticity and depth.
- Assuming no policy means no rules. The Common App still requires certifying the work is your own.
- UC runs plagiarism checks, and AI-generated personal insight answers can mean disqualification from UC admission entirely.
