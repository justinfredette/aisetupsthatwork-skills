# Build your case for a raise or promotion

## When to use

Employees who have earned more but dread the conversation and want a plan.

## Steps

1. Research the market range for your position, experience level, and location. Note the number you can defend.
2. Build a one-page brag sheet: what you achieved this year, times you went above and beyond, what you plan next.
3. Decide your specific ask. A precise number shows research; a range invites the bottom of it.
4. Paste your brag sheet into the prompt below for a practice script and answers to likely pushback.
5. Schedule a real meeting. Do not ambush your manager in a hallway.
6. If the answer is no or later, ask about alternate benefits, and get any future commitment in writing with clear targets.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
I want to ask for a raise. My role: [ROLE], location: [CITY]. Market research shows [RANGE]. My achievements this year: [PASTE BRAG SHEET]. Draft: 1) a short opening script for the meeting that states a specific target number and ties it to my contributions; 2) calm replies to three likely pushbacks (budget freeze, "you are already at range", "let us revisit later"); 3) three alternate asks if salary cannot move (PTO, bonus, flexible schedule). Keep the tone collaborative, never threatening. Show me the scripts.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI cannot verify your market data. Bring real numbers from a salary source.
- Never threaten to quit if you do not get what you want. It ends the conversation, not wins it.
- A verbal "we will revisit this" is worth nothing. Get commitments in writing with measurable targets.
