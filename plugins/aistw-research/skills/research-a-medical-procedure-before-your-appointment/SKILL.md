# Research a medical procedure before your appointment

## When to use

Anyone facing a surgery or procedure who wants to understand it and ask good questions at the appointment.

## Steps

1. Write down the exact procedure name from your doctor. Spelling matters here.
2. Ask the AI to explain the procedure in plain language: what happens, why it is done, and the alternatives.
3. Ask for the standard risks, benefits, and recovery timeline from reputable medical sources.
4. Ask the AI to build a question list for your appointment: surgeon experience, board certification, anesthesia, costs, second opinions.
5. Print or save the question list and bring it. People forget half their questions in the room.
6. After the appointment, compare what your doctor said against the briefing and follow up on anything that conflicts.
7. Make the decision with your doctor, not with the AI. The AI prepares you; it does not diagnose or recommend.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
My doctor recommended this procedure and I want to understand it before my appointment:

Procedure: [EXACT NAME]
Reason: [DIAGNOSIS OR SYMPTOMS, AS YOUR DOCTOR DESCRIBED THEM]

Please:
1. Explain the procedure in plain language: what happens, why it is done, and how long it takes.
2. List the standard benefits, risks, and alternatives, based on reputable medical sources like Mayo Clinic.
3. Describe the typical recovery timeline: hospital stay, pain, activity limits, and follow-up.
4. Build me a question list for my appointment covering: the surgeon’s experience and board certification, anesthesia type, total cost and insurance coverage, whether a second opinion makes sense, and warning signs after the procedure.
5. Tell me what to bring and how to prepare.

Important: you are helping me prepare questions, not giving medical advice. If anything is uncertain, say so and tell me to ask my doctor.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI is not a doctor and its medical knowledge can be outdated or wrong. Every claim needs your doctor’s confirmation.
- AI answers can sound confident about rare complications or new techniques. Treat confidence as a warning sign, not proof.
- Do not paste detailed personal health records into a public AI tool without thinking about privacy first.
