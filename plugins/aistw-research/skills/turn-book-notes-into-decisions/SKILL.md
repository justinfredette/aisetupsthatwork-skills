# Turn book notes into decisions instead of a highlight graveyard

## When to use

Readers with a notes app full of highlights that never changed anything.

## Steps

1. Export your highlights or paste your chapter notes into the AI.
2. Tell the AI what decisions you are facing at work or in life right now.
3. Ask it to extract the three to five ideas most relevant to those decisions. Not a summary, a shortlist.
4. For each idea, ask for one concrete action: what to try, by when, and how you will know it worked.
5. Ask for a one-page reference: the idea, why it matters to you, and the action. Save it where you will see it.
6. Schedule a 30-day review. Ask the AI to turn your notes into review questions for that date.
7. Delete or archive the rest. A highlight you will never act on is clutter with good branding.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
I finished a book and took notes, but I want decisions, not a summary.

Book: [TITLE AND AUTHOR]
My notes and highlights:
[PASTE NOTES]

Decisions I am currently facing:
[LIST 2 TO 4 REAL DECISIONS, e.g. pricing change, hiring, morning routine]

Please:
1. Pull out the three to five ideas from my notes most relevant to these decisions. Ignore the rest.
2. For each idea: restate it in one sentence, say why it matters for my specific decision, and give me one concrete action with a deadline.
3. Build a one-page reference I can revisit: idea, why it matters, action, and how I will know it worked.
4. Write five review questions I can use in 30 days to test whether I actually applied any of this.

Do not summarize the whole book. Focus only on what connects to my decisions. If none of my notes connect, tell me that plainly.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI will happily summarize instead of deciding. Keep redirecting it to actions tied to your decisions.
- Vague actions like "communicate better" die on contact. Demand a concrete first step with a date.
- If your notes are just quotes with no context, the AI has little to work with. Add a line about why each quote mattered.
