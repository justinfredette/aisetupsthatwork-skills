# Start a Business: LLC or Sole Proprietor Setup Checklist

## When to use

First-time founders choosing a business structure. This is not legal or tax advice; check with a licensed professional before you file anything.

## Steps

1. Learn the core tradeoff: a sole proprietorship may be the easiest structure to form, but you are personally responsible for all business losses and liabilities, while an LLC separates business and personal liabilities.
2. Read the IRS definitions of each structure so you understand how each is taxed.
3. Get an Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS. You will use it to open a bank account, apply for licenses, and pay federal taxes.
4. Check federal, state, county, and local governments for the permits or licenses your business needs.
5. Open a separate business bank account. Do not mix personal and business funds.
6. Use the AI to turn your notes into a checklist with your state’s steps in order, then review every line yourself.
7. Talk to a tax or legal professional, or a free SCORE or SBDC counselor, before you file.

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI cannot give legal or tax advice and may state rules that do not apply in your state. Verify on official sites.
- Do not paste your Social Security number or EIN into a chatbot. You do not need them to build the checklist.
- A sole proprietorship means personal liability for business debts. Understand that before choosing simple over protected.
- Some sites charge fees for free filings like EINs. Get the EIN from the IRS directly.
