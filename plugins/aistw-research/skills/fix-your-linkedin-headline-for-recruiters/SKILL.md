# Fix your LinkedIn headline for recruiters

## When to use

Job seekers whose LinkedIn profile reads fine to humans but never surfaces in recruiter searches.

## Steps

1. Collect 5-10 target job postings. List the skills and titles that repeat across them.
2. Paste your work history and the keyword list into the prompt below for headline options.
3. Pick the formula: current or desired title | key skills | one-line value proposition.
4. Rewrite the About section to tell the story behind the headline: who you help and what they can count on.
5. Cut vague phrases like "results-driven professional." Concrete language about your role and impact wins.
6. Update the headline in your profile and check it on mobile. Most recruiters skim on phones.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Here is my work history: [PASTE]. These are the keywords from job postings I want: [PASTE LIST]. Draft 3 LinkedIn headline options using the formula: title | key skills | value proposition, separated by vertical bars. Each under 220 characters. Then draft a 3-paragraph About section that says who I help and what they can count on me to deliver. Do not invent results, metrics, or credentials I did not provide. Show me the drafts.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI will invent impressive metrics if you let it. Forbid invented results in the prompt and verify every claim.
- A keyword-stuffed headline reads as spam to humans. Keep it to title, skills, value prop.
- The About section is not a resume repeat. Tell the story the bullet points cannot.
