# Plan a Home Renovation With AI: Scope, Budget, and Contractor Quotes

## When to use

Homeowners planning a renovation who want to avoid vague quotes and surprise costs.

## Steps

1. Describe the project to AI: the room, what you want changed, your must-haves and nice-to-haves. Get back a line-item scope: demolition, materials, labor, fixtures, finishes.
2. Lock in specifics before asking for prices. As This Old House puts it, the more details nailed down upfront, the fewer costly surprises later.
3. Ask AI to draft a bid request you can send to contractors: same scope, same specs, so bids are comparable.
4. Get at least three written, itemized bids. The FTC and consumer guides agree: compare line by line, and throw out bids significantly lower than the others.
5. Check each contractor: license with the state board, proof of liability and workers' comp insurance verified with the insurer, references, and a business-name search with "complaint" or "scam".
6. Sign a written contract with every promise in it before work starts. Pay by check or card, never cash, and never pay in full until the work is done and approved.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me plan a home renovation. Plain and practical.

Project: [e.g. kitchen refresh, 120 sq ft]
What I want: [e.g. new cabinets, counters, keep the layout]
Must-haves: [list]
Nice-to-haves: [list]
Rough budget: [range]

Write:
1. A line-item scope: demolition, materials, labor, fixtures, finishes. Flag what I have not decided yet.
2. A bid request I can send to contractors: same specs for everyone, asking for itemized pricing.
3. A bid comparison checklist: what to compare line by line, and red flags in a bid.
4. A contractor vetting checklist: license, insurance, references, payment terms.

Rules:
- No cost estimates presented as facts unless you say they are rough ranges. I will get real bids.
- Include the FTC red flags: cash-only, full payment upfront, door-to-door solicitation, pressure tactics.
- Show me the draft first. I will review before sending anything to contractors.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Vague scopes produce vague bids, then change orders. Lock details before pricing.
- The lowball bid. Significantly cheaper than the rest usually means cut corners or desperation.
- Handshake deals. The FTC is blunt: get everything in writing or it did not happen.
- Paying in full upfront. Standard practice is a deposit with the balance on completion and approval.
