# Onboard your first employees

## When to use

First-time employers hiring employee number one (or two) with no HR department.

## Steps

1. Confirm your employer obligations: federal EIN, state new-hire reporting, and workers comp. Check your state labor site; do not guess.
2. Use the prompt below to draft a role description with specific tasks and expectations for the first three months.
3. Build the paperwork packet: W-4, I-9, direct deposit details, and any state forms. Collect originals on day one.
4. Write a two-week training plan that mixes shadowing you, small solo tasks, and a go-to person for questions.
5. Schedule a weekly 15-minute check-in for the first month. Feedback early beats corrections late.
6. File the state new hire report on time and keep copies of everything signed.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
I am hiring my first employee as a [ROLE] at a small [TYPE OF BUSINESS]. Draft: 1) a role description with specific tasks, responsibilities, and measurable expectations for the first three months; 2) a two-week onboarding checklist (paperwork, training steps, who teaches what); 3) five weekly check-in questions for the first month. Keep it practical for a business with no HR department. Do not include legal advice; flag anything I should confirm with my state labor site. Show me the drafts.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI cannot tell you your state filing deadlines or forms. Confirm every compliance step on your state labor site.
- Do not paste employee personal details (SSN, bank numbers) into a chatbot. Collect those on paper or your payroll system.
- A checklist is not training. Shadowing and feedback do the real work.
