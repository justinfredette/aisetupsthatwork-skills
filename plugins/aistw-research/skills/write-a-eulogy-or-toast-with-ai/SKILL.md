# Write a Eulogy or Toast With AI Without Losing Your Voice

## When to use

Anyone asked to speak at a funeral or wedding who is staring at a blank page.

## Steps

1. Write down 2 to 3 specific stories first, before any drafting. Specific moments beat general praise: the story proves "she was kind" better than the sentence does.
2. Pick the structure: opening and your relationship, who they were, the stories, what they taught you, gratitude and farewell.
3. Draft it in your own words, aiming for 3 to 7 minutes (about 400 to 900 spoken words). Skip the resume-style biography: dates and job titles summarize, stories show.
4. Ask AI for feedback only: where it drags, where the emotion lands, whether the closing earns its weight. No AI-written sentences in the final version.
5. Read every draft aloud. Rewrite anything that stumbles. Your ear is the editor.
6. Print in large font, number the pages, bring water and tissues, and name a backup reader in case you cannot finish.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me structure a eulogy. Feedback and structure only: do not write it for me.

Who: [relationship, e.g. my grandmother]
Tone they would want: [e.g. warm with some humor, not somber]
My stories: [2-3 specific memories, in my own words]
Length target: [e.g. 5 minutes]

Give me:
1. A structure outline: opening, story placement, closing. Fit my stories into it.
2. Feedback on my draft below: where it drags, where a transition fails, whether the closing lands. Point at problems; do not rewrite sentences.
3. A delivery checklist: printing, pacing, what to do if I break down.

Rules:
- NEVER write sentences for me to read aloud. This must be 100% my words and my memories.
- Flag anything that sounds generic enough that anyone could have written it.
- Show me the feedback. I will do all writing myself.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Letting AI write sentences. A eulogy in anyone else's words, human or AI, cannot carry your memories.
- The full biography. Nobody needs the resume: two or three true stories say more.
- Private or painful details. Honor the person; do not settle scores or overshare.
- Memorizing it. Print it large, number pages, and read. Nerves erase memorized text.
