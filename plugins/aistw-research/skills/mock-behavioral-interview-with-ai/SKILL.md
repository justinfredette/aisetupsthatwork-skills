# Mock behavioral interview with AI

## When to use

Job seekers who want to practice "tell me about a time" questions and get honest feedback before the real thing.

## Steps

1. Paste the job description and your resume into the prompt below. Name the role, industry, and the soft skills being tested.
2. Tell the AI to ask one behavioral question at a time and wait for your answer before continuing.
3. Answer out loud, from real experience, in 60-90 seconds. Type a short version of what you said.
4. Ask the AI to score the answer against a simple rubric: specific example, your role, result, and relevance to the job.
5. Have it flag filler words and vague phrases, and suggest exact replacement wording.
6. Repeat for 5-8 questions, then ask for the two weakest answers and redo them.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Act as an interviewer for this role: [ROLE] at a [INDUSTRY] company. Here is the job description: [PASTE]. Here is my resume: [PASTE]. Ask me one behavioral interview question at a time. Wait for my answer before asking the next. After each answer, score it 1-5 on: specific example, my clear role, measurable result, relevance to this job. Point out filler words and vague phrases with exact replacements. Start with question one.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI feedback is only as good as your input. Paste the real job description or the rubric is generic.
- Typing answers is easier than saying them. Practice out loud at least once before the interview.
- AI can be overly encouraging. Ask directly: "where was this answer weak?"
