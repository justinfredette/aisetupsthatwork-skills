# Do due diligence on a job offer before you sign

## When to use

Anyone with an offer letter who wants to know what it is really worth before signing.

## Steps

1. Get the offer in writing with the benefits summary. Never evaluate a verbal offer.
2. Paste the details into the prompt below: base, bonus, equity, PTO, health costs, retirement match.
3. Ask the AI to convert everything to an annualized total compensation number.
4. Ask it to check the salary against market ranges for the role, level, and location.
5. Ask for red flags: vague equity terms, clawback clauses, below-market PTO, high health premiums.
6. Use the AI’s negotiation points to draft your counter. Ask for what the data supports.
7. Get every agreed change back in writing before you resign anywhere.
8. Set three numbers before the call: your walk-away number, your target, and the number you will actually say first (above target, still credible). Then ask for a counter script in four parts: enthusiasm about the role, one specific number, one or two reasons tied to market data and your experience, and flexibility on other items. Review the script before the call; you say the words.
9. Ask for a second script for when base will not move: a signing bonus, a written salary review at six months with criteria in the offer, or extra leave. When they answer 'that is above the band,' ask what the top of the band is, then pivot to the non-base items. Follow any agreed change with an email restating the new terms and asking for the updated offer in writing.
10. Thank them and ask for a few days to review. Get the full compensation details in writing before you counter.
11. Look up market pay for the role, level, and city. Note the number you can defend.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
I received a job offer and want to evaluate the total compensation before I respond.

Offer details:
- Role and level: [TITLE, LEVEL]
- Location (or remote): [LOCATION]
- Base salary: [AMOUNT]
- Signing bonus: [AMOUNT, and any clawback terms]
- Annual bonus: [TARGET PERCENT OR AMOUNT]
- Equity: [TYPE AND AMOUNT, vesting schedule]
- PTO and holidays: [DAYS]
- Health insurance: [MONTHLY PREMIUM AND DEDUCTIBLE, if known]
- Retirement match: [DETAILS]
- Other perks: [LIST]

My current situation: [CURRENT SALARY AND BENEFITS, OR "this is my first offer"]

Please:
1. Convert everything to an annualized total compensation figure and show your math.
2. Compare the base salary to market range for this role, level, and location.
3. Flag red flags: vague terms, clawbacks, weak benefits, or anything unusual.
4. Give me three to five specific negotiation points backed by the numbers.
5. Tell me what questions to ask HR before I decide.

Do not invent market data. If you are unsure about a range, say so and tell me where to check.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI market salary data can be outdated or thin for niche roles. Cross-check with levels.fyi, Glassdoor, or recent postings.
- The AI cannot read your actual benefits booklet. Get the real documents from HR.
- Do not paste anything covered by an NDA, and keep personal identifiers out of the prompt.
- Do not justify the ask with personal needs like rent or childcare - employers pay for market value, not circumstances. And do not name your current salary to anchor the number; use market data instead.
