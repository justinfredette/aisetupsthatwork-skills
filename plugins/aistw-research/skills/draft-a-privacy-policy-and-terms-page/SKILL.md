# Draft a Privacy Policy and Terms Page for Your Site

## When to use

Site owners who need policy pages before launch. This is not legal advice; a lawyer must review the final pages.

## Steps

1. Inventory your data first: list everything you collect, why you collect it, and who else sees it. The FTC’s first principle is to take stock of what personal information you have.
2. Cut anything you do not need. Keeping only what you need is both safer and easier to disclose.
3. Draft the privacy policy sections: who you are, what you collect, how and why you use each category, who you share it with, user rights, cookies, security, retention, and children’s privacy.
4. Draft the terms sections: acceptable use, intellectual property, user content, limitation of liability, termination, and governing law.
5. Add a visible last-updated date to the privacy policy.
6. Use clickwrap for terms: a checkbox or button where users affirmatively agree, which courts favor over a passive link.
7. Have a lawyer review both pages against your actual practices, then publish and update them whenever your billing, features, or data practices change.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me draft a privacy policy and terms of service for my website. These are DRAFTS for a lawyer to review, not final legal documents.

What my site does: [one paragraph]
Data I collect: [forms, analytics, cookies, payments, accounts: be specific]
Third parties who see data: [analytics provider, payment processor, email tool, etc.]
Do I serve children under 13: [yes/no]
State or country: [location]

Rules:
- Privacy policy sections: business identity and scope, categories of data collected, how and why each is used, sharing and third parties, user rights, cookies and tracking, security, retention, children’s privacy, last-updated date.
- Terms sections: acceptable use, intellectual property, user-generated content, limitation of liability, termination, governing law.
- Describe only what I listed. Do not invent data practices.
- Flag every section a lawyer must customize with "[LAWYER REVIEW]".
- End with: "Have an attorney review and tailor these to your actual business practices before publishing."

Review the drafts before sending them to a lawyer.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI-drafted legal pages are not reviewed and not tailored to your business. They must go through a lawyer before publishing.
- Do not paste customer data or real user records into a chatbot while drafting examples.
- Terms need a governing law and liability section that fits your state. Generic language may not hold up.
- If your site collects any personal data, privacy rules like state privacy laws may apply. The draft is a starting point, not compliance.
