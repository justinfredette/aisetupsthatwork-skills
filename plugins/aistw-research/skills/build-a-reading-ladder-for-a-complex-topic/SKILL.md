# Build a reading ladder to learn a complex topic fast

## When to use

Professionals who need to get smart on a hard topic quickly and cannot afford to read in the wrong order.

## Steps

1. Define the topic narrowly and state your deadline and why you are learning it.
2. Tell the AI your starting level honestly. The ladder depends on it.
3. Ask for a reading ladder: 5 to 8 rungs from accessible explainers up to primary sources, with what each rung teaches.
4. Ask the AI to include one "story around the topic" per rung. Stories stick better than textbooks alone.
5. Read rung one, then explain it back to the AI in your own words. If you cannot, do not climb yet.
6. Ask the AI for check questions after each rung before moving up.
7. Keep a one-page running summary as you climb. At the top, you should be able to brief someone else.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
I need to learn a complex topic quickly. Build me a reading ladder.

Topic: [SPECIFIC TOPIC]
Why I need it: [e.g. client project, new role, investment decision]
My current level: [e.g. total beginner, familiar with basics, intermediate]
Deadline: [DATE]
Time I can spend per day: [e.g. 45 minutes]

Build a ladder of 5 to 8 rungs, easiest first:
- For each rung: title and author, what it is (book, paper, explainer, video), what it will teach me, and why it comes at this rung.
- Include accessible explainers and at least one engaging "story around the topic" before the dense material.
- End with the primary sources or key papers people in the field actually cite.
- After each rung, give me three check questions to test whether I am ready to climb.

Only recommend real books, papers, and resources. If you are unsure one exists, leave it out and say so.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI can recommend books or papers that do not exist. Verify every title before buying or citing it.
- Ladders drift toward the AI’s favorite pop-science books. Push it for primary sources at the top rungs.
- Reading without the check questions is just entertainment. Do the retrieval practice or the ladder is decorative.
