# Write your self review from your notes

## When to use

Employees facing performance review season with a pile of notes and no idea how to start.

## Steps

1. Dump everything into one doc: projects shipped, problems solved, praise received, with dates and numbers where you have them.
2. For team wins, write down your specific contribution. "My part was..." beats "we did..."
3. Paste the notes into the prompt below for a structured draft: opening, accomplishments, growth areas, goals, close.
4. Replace every hedged phrase ("I think I helped") with the plain fact ("I cut report time from 3 days to 1").
5. Add one or two honest growth areas, each with an action already underway.
6. Read it as your manager would. Cut anything that needs insider context to understand.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Here are my notes from this review period: [PASTE PROJECTS, WINS, FEEDBACK, DATES]. My role is [ROLE] and my team goals are [GOALS]. Draft a self-review with: 1) a two-sentence opening, 2) accomplishments with specific examples and numbers where my notes have them, naming my personal contribution in team efforts, 3) one or two honest growth areas with an action underway for each, 4) goals for next period tied to team objectives, 5) a forward-looking close. State accomplishments as facts, no hedging, no exaggeration beyond my notes. Show me the draft.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI cannot verify your wins. Every number in the draft must come from your notes.
- Over-reliance on AI produces a generic review that could belong to anyone. Your specifics are the value.
- Skipping growth areas reads as unaware. One honest area with a plan builds more trust than perfection.
