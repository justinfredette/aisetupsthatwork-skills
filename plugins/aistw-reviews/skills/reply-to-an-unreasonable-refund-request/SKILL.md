# Reply to an unreasonable refund request

## When to use

Small business owners who get angry refund demands that fall outside their stated policy.

## Steps

1. Read the demand once and note which part of your policy it falls outside of.
2. Decide the outcome before drafting: decline, partial credit, or redo. Do not negotiate with yourself in the reply.
3. Paste the customer message and your policy into the prompt below to get a calm decline plus an alternative offer.
4. Read the draft out loud. Cut anything defensive or sarcastic before sending.
5. Send it yourself, then log the exchange in one line (date, customer, outcome) for your records.
6. If they threaten a chargeback, keep all messages and your policy page; do not escalate in writing.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
A customer wrote: [PASTE THEIR MESSAGE]. My refund policy says: [PASTE POLICY OR DESCRIBE IT]. The refund they want is not covered. Draft a reply that: 1) acknowledges their frustration in one sentence, 2) declines the refund clearly and points to the policy, 3) offers one concrete alternative from this list: redo the work, a discount on a future service, store credit. Keep it under 120 words, professional, no blame. Show me the draft before I send anything.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI does not know your policy. Paste it, or the draft will invent terms you never offered.
- Copy-pasting a script word for word can read as robotic. Adjust one line so it sounds like you.
- Never promise a refund "just this once" in writing unless you mean it as policy.
