# Write Reviews That Actually Help People

## When to use

People who want to support good businesses and warn about bad ones, but write "Great place!" and feel it says nothing.

## Steps

1. Right after the visit, jot raw notes: what you got, when, who served you, one good thing, one bad thing.
2. Paste the notes into chat and ask for a review under 150 words: specific, balanced, conversational.
3. Check that every specific claim is true. AI must not invent dishes, prices, or staff names.
4. Add one photo if you have a good one. Photos make reviews far more trusted.
5. Post it yourself on Google Maps or Yelp. Disclose any connection to the business.
6. Update it later if things change. An updated review reads as fair, not fickle.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Turn my rough notes into a helpful online review. Conversational, specific, honest.

Business: [name, type]
My notes: [what I got, when, what stood out, what did not]

Write a review under 150 words:
- Open with the overall verdict in one sentence.
- Name specifics: dishes, services, staff, prices, wait times.
- Include one honest caveat if there was one. Balanced reviews are trusted more.
- Close with who this place suits.

Rules:
- Use only my notes. Never invent details, prices, or names.
- No gushing superlatives, no trashing. Plainspoken.
- Write like a person telling a friend, not a brochure.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI will invent specifics if your notes are thin. "Great food" in, invented dish names out. Check everything.
- Never review places you did not visit, and never let an employer pressure you into 5 stars. Both violate policy.
- Disclose connections. Worked there? Related to the owner? Say so.
- All-caps rants get skimmed, not trusted. Calm specifics win.
