# Dispute a Fake or Unfair Review (and Know When to Let It Go)

## When to use

Business owners staring at a review from someone who was never a customer, a competitor, or an ex-employee with a grudge.

## Steps

1. Check the reviewer’s profile first: new account, one review, reviews in five cities in one day. Screenshot everything.
2. Compare the review against your records: names, dates, orders. Note exactly what does not match.
3. Match it to a Google policy category: fake content, conflict of interest, harassment, off-topic, or spam.
4. Report it: Business Profile, Read reviews, Report review, pick the closest category. Submit.
5. Track the status in Google’s Reviews Management Tool: pending, no violation found, or escalated.
6. If denied, appeal once with your evidence file. If the appeal fails and the review is from a real customer, write a professional reply and move on.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me build an evidence file to report a suspicious Google review. Be precise and factual.

Business: [name]
Review text: [paste]
Reviewer profile notes: [account age, other reviews, anything odd]
My records: [why I believe this is not a real customer: no matching name, date, order]

Return:
1. The Google policy category this most likely violates, and why in one sentence.
2. A bullet evidence list I can attach: screenshots to take, records to pull.
3. Tight report wording under 100 words: factual, no emotion, no insults.
4. Your honest assessment: strong case, weak case, or not a policy violation at all.

Rules:
- If this looks like a real customer with a real complaint, say so plainly and tell me to reply instead of report.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- A harsh review from a real customer is almost never removed. "Unfair" is not a policy violation.
- Paid removal services promise what they cannot control. Google decides, and nobody can guarantee it.
- Extortion (pay me or the review stays) has a separate Google reporting route. Do not engage or pay.
- Expect days to weeks, not hours. The status tool will say pending for a while.
