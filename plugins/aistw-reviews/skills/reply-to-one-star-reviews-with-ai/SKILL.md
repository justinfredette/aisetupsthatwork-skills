# Reply to 1-Star Reviews Without Sounding Like a Robot

## When to use

Small business owners who stare at a bad review for an hour, then either ignore it or reply while angry.

## Steps

1. Wait until you are calm. Never draft a reply in the first hour after reading a bad review.
2. Paste the full review text and star rating into chat, plus the date if shown.
3. Paste your fix facts: what you honestly offer (a redo, a refund, a phone call). If you offer nothing, say so plainly.
4. Ask for one reply under 70 words: thank them for the feedback, apologize for their experience once, offer the one fix, give a phone number or email.
5. Read the draft out loud. If it sounds like a press release, ask for a plainer version.
6. Post it yourself on the review platform. Do not let any tool auto-post as your business.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Draft a public reply to this 1-star review for my small business. I will post it myself.

Review: [paste full text]
Rating: [1 or 2 stars]

What I can actually offer to fix it: [one real thing: redo, refund, callback, or "nothing beyond an apology"]
My contact for follow-up: [phone or email]
Tone: calm, human, plainspoken. No corporate speak.

Rules:
- Apologize for their experience exactly once.
- Offer the one fix listed above, nothing more.
- Invite them to contact me directly. Do not argue about what happened.
- Under 70 words. Sign with [your first name].
- Never invent policies, discounts, or freebies I did not list.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI drafts lean corporate ("We sincerely regret any inconvenience"). Ask for plainer words.
- The model may invent a refund or freebie you never offered. Check every promise against your real policy.
- Do not confirm private details (order numbers, amounts) in a public reply.
- If you reply while angry, even a good draft will get a sharp edit from you. Sleep on it.
