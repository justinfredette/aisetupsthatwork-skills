# Ask Happy Customers for Reviews at the Right Moment

## When to use

Small business owners with great service and twelve reviews, because asking feels awkward and keeps slipping the mind.

## Steps

1. Write down your three best ask moments: when the job wraps, when a customer compliments you, when a problem gets fixed.
2. Get your direct Google review link from your Business Profile on a desktop browser.
3. Ask AI for three short request scripts: one for text, one for email, one to say in person. Keep each under 40 words.
4. Make the scripts specific to your moments ("Since the furnace is running again...").
5. Attach the ask to the milestone, not your memory. Invoice sent, ask sent. Job done, ask sent.
6. If someone says yes but does not post, send one gentle reminder with the link. Then drop it.
7. Pick 2-3 trigger moments when you ask every time: job done, invoice paid, compliment received. Same milestone for everyone, so it becomes habit and stays fair.
8. Ask AI to draft a short ask message from your trigger list, with [bracketed] spots for name, the specific moment, and your form link. Link to a 3-question form (a Google Form works): what problem did they have before, what result did they get, would they recommend you and why. Show output first, then send it yourself. Store every answer in one spreadsheet with the client name and date.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Write short review-request messages for my small business. Plainspoken, no hype.

Business: [name, what you do]
Ask moments: [e.g. job finished, checkout, delivery day]
Review link: [paste your Google review link]

Write three versions, each under 40 words:
1. A text message to send within a few hours of the job.
2. A short email for the day after.
3. One sentence to say in person at handoff.

Rules:
- Thank them first, ask second.
- Ask for an honest review, never for 5 stars specifically.
- No discounts, prizes, or incentives for reviewing.
- Make it easy: mention it takes about a minute.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Never offer discounts or prizes for reviews. Google removes incentivized reviews and can suspend your profile.
- Do not hand a customer your phone and watch them write it. A QR card on the counter is fine; hovering is pressure.
- Asking "a week later" barely works. Same day beats next week every time.
- Staff will forget. Tie the ask to a step that already happens, like the invoice.
- Collecting a great testimonial you cannot publish is wasted effort. Ask 'may I share this with your name' inside the form itself.
