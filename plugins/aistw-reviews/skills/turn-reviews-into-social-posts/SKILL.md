# Turn Good Reviews Into Social Media Posts

## When to use

Small business owners sitting on dozens of glowing reviews who post "Just a reminder we are open!" because content ideas run dry.

## Steps

1. Pick your 10 strongest reviews: specific, enthusiastic, naming a product, service, or person.
2. Paste them into chat in a batch and ask for one social post per review: a short quote pull, a caption under 60 words, and 3 hashtags.
3. Match the format to the platform: square quote graphic for Instagram, plain quote plus caption for Facebook, short story version for LinkedIn.
4. Build the graphics in Canva with your brand colors. Keep the reviewer’s words exact.
5. Get permission before using a customer’s full name or photo. First name plus initial is the safe default.
6. Schedule one review post per week. Mix them with your normal content so the feed is not all testimonials.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Turn these customer reviews into social media posts for my business. I will design and publish them myself.

Business: [name, type]
Brand voice: [e.g. warm, plainspoken, a little funny]

Reviews:
[paste reviews, numbered]

For each review, give me:
1. A quote pull under 20 words (exact customer words, no edits that change meaning).
2. A caption under 60 words that adds context: what the review was about, a thank-you, one soft call to action.
3. Three relevant hashtags.

Rules:
- Never invent details the reviewer did not mention.
- If a review is long, pull the strongest sentence; do not stitch sentences together to say something new.
- Keep my brand voice. No hype words.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Editing a quote to sound better can misrepresent the customer. Pull exact words or paraphrase openly.
- Using full names or photos without permission backfires. Ask first; most happy customers say yes.
- Posting five review graphics in a row looks desperate. One a week, mixed with real content.
- Screenshots of reviews with personal info visible (reviewer photos, locations) need a crop check.
