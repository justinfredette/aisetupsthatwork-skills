# Thank-You Replies for 5-Star Reviews That Do Not Sound Canned

## When to use

Business owners who reply "Thank you for your review! We appreciate your business!" to everyone and know readers can tell.

## Steps

1. Collect 5 to 10 recent positive reviews you have not answered yet.
2. Paste them into chat in one batch with your business name and tone (warm, brief).
3. Ask for one reply per review, each under 50 words, each mentioning one specific detail from that review.
4. Demand variety: no two replies may open with the same sentence.
5. Edit each draft: fix names, fix details, sign with your name.
6. Post them yourself, a few per day rather than all at once.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Draft thank-you replies to these 5-star reviews for my business. I will edit and post each one myself.

Business: [name]
Tone: [warm and brief / friendly / professional]
Sign-off name: [your first name]

Reviews:
[paste 5-10 reviews, numbered]

Rules:
- One reply per review, under 50 words each.
- Use the reviewer's name. Mention ONE specific detail from their review (a dish, a service, a staff member).
- No two replies may start with the same words.
- No discount codes, no promotions, no keyword stuffing.
- Plainspoken and human. Never start with "Thank you for your review! We appreciate your business!"
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Copy-paste replies are obvious to anyone who scrolls your reviews. Vary every single one.
- Do not stuff keywords ("best plumber in Austin!"). It reads as spam and does nothing for ranking.
- Skip the promo code in a thank-you. It turns gratitude into an ad.
- AI sometimes thanks the reviewer for details they never mentioned. Verify before posting.
