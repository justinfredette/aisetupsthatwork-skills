# How to Use AI to Reply to Google Reviews (Even the Bad Ones)

## When to use

Small business owners who know they should answer reviews but stall on the angry ones, or who copy-paste the same “thanks!” line until it sounds robotic.

## Steps

1. Copy the review text and star rating into chat. Include the date if Google shows it.
2. Paste your fact sheet and tone rules (warm, brief, no legal threats, no bribing for edits).
3. Ask for two drafts under 80 words: one public reply, one optional private follow-up note you might send by email.
4. For 1–2 star reviews, require empathy, one specific fix you can offer, and an offline contact path. No arguing in public.
5. Read for HIPAA, PCI, or client confidentiality if the review names a person or order.
6. Paste into Google Business Profile and post yourself; do not connect send or GBP write access to an agent.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
You draft Google review replies for my business. Do not post to Google or send email unless I paste your text myself.

Business facts (only use these: do not invent policies):
[paste name, location, phone, hours, real redo/refund policy]

Tone: [friendly and brief / formal / apologetic but firm]
Max length: 80 words per public reply

Review to answer:
Rating: [1-5 stars]
Text: [paste review]

Rules:
- Thank the reviewer by name if shown.
- For low stars: apologize for their experience, offer one concrete next step we can actually do, invite them to call/email: no discounts unless listed in business facts.
- For high stars: thank them and mention one specific detail from their review.
- No legal threats, no asking them to change the review, no sharing private customer data.
- Output public reply only unless I ask for a private follow-up draft.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Drafts can sound corporate or passive-aggressive. Read aloud before you post.
- Never confirm private order details in a public reply; keep it vague and move offline.
- Google’s policies still apply to your text. AI does not know your industry’s compliance rules.
