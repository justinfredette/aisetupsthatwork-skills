# Write a Rental Reference Letter With AI

## When to use

Landlords asked for a reference letter, and renters who need one and want to make it easy for a busy landlord to say yes.

## Steps

1. Gather the facts first: exact tenancy dates, whether rent was on time, condition at move-out, any complaints or violations.
2. Decide your honest recommendation level: rent again gladly, rent with reservations, or decline to write the letter.
3. Paste the facts into chat and ask for a one-page formal letter with a clear recommendation sentence.
4. Read every sentence for accuracy. AI will happily invent dates and details if you leave gaps.
5. Strip anything about family, health, background, or personal life. Rental facts only.
6. Sign it yourself if you are the landlord. If you are the tenant, send the draft to your landlord and let them edit and sign.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Draft a landlord reference letter. Formal, concise, one page. Only use the facts I give you. Do not invent anything.

Landlord name: [your name]
Landlord phone/email: [contact]
Tenant full name: [name]
Property address: [address]
Tenancy dates: [start - end]
Rent payment history: [e.g. always on time / two late payments in 2023]
Property condition: [e.g. excellent, normal wear / describe any damage]
Lease issues: [none / describe briefly]
Would rent again: [yes / with reservations]

Rules:
- Mention only rental facts: payments, condition, communication, lease compliance.
- No comments on family, health, background, or personal characteristics.
- End with a clear recommendation sentence and an offer to be contacted.
- If any fact is missing, leave a [bracket] for me instead of guessing.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI invents specifics when facts are missing. Fill every bracket yourself before sending.
- Mentioning protected characteristics (race, family status, religion, disability) creates Fair Housing exposure, even as a compliment. Leave it all out.
- Do not write a glowing letter for a tenant who trashed the place just to move them along. The next landlord will remember.
- Tenants: never sign your landlord’s name. Draft it, send it, let them edit and sign.
