# Your Reputation Command Center: Monitor, Respond, Improve

## When to use

Established small businesses with 50-plus reviews who want reputation handled systematically instead of in crisis mode.

## Steps

1. Baseline once: record your rating and review count on each platform, plus your listing accuracy (hours, phone, address).
2. Monthly, pull the numbers: new reviews, average rating, response rate, response time.
3. Sweep unanswered reviews and draft replies in one batch. Nothing older than 30 days stays unanswered.
4. Mine the month’s reviews for themes: top complaint, top praise, anything new.
5. Pick one operational fix from the themes and assign it an owner and a date.
6. Write the one-page monthly brief: numbers, themes, the fix, next month’s watch item. File it where you will find it.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Run my monthly reputation audit. I will verify the numbers before acting on them.

Business: [name, locations]
Platforms: [Google, Yelp, Facebook, others]

This month's numbers:
- New reviews per platform and average rating: [paste]
- Unanswered reviews: [paste with dates]
- Last month's baseline: [rating and count per platform]

Return a one-page brief:
1. Scorecard: rating now vs last month, review velocity, response rate.
2. Reply drafts for unanswered reviews, under 60 words each.
3. Top complaint theme and top praise theme with 2 quotes each.
4. One recommended operational fix with an owner and a deadline.
5. One watch item for next month.

Rules:
- Flag anything that looks fake for reporting, not replying.
- Do not invent numbers. If I did not provide a baseline, say so.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Tool sprawl: three dashboards and no habit. Start with a spreadsheet and a calendar block.
- Vanity metrics: total review count going up while the same complaint repeats for six months.
- Skipping the ops fix. The audit is only worth it if something in the business changes.
- Ignoring industry sites. Your Google looks great; your Healthgrades or TripAdvisor tells another story.
