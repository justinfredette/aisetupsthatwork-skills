# Restaurant Review Replies: Templates for Yelp, Google, and TripAdvisor

## When to use

Restaurant, cafe, bar, and food truck owners who get reviewed constantly and need replies that sound like a human who was actually there.

## Steps

1. List your six situations: rave about food, rave about service, mixed 3-star, bad food, bad service, suspicious or fake.
2. Ask AI for one template per situation, each under 70 words, in your restaurant’s voice.
3. Add your specifics: signature dishes, your name, the phone number for offline follow-up.
4. For each new review, pick the closest template and rewrite it around one detail from the actual review.
5. Post it yourself. Never auto-post, and never let the template show: change the opener every time.
6. Once a month, read your last 20 replies in a row. If they blur together, refresh the templates.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Write restaurant review reply templates for me. I will personalize each one before posting.

Restaurant: [name, cuisine, vibe: e.g. family Italian, casual]
My name and role: [e.g. Maria, owner]
Contact for unhappy guests: [phone or email]
Signature dishes: [2-3]
Real policies: [e.g. we redo a dish on the spot; no cash refunds after the visit]

Write six templates, each under 70 words:
1. 5-star praising the food.
2. 5-star praising the service or a named staffer.
3. 3-star mixed (liked X, did not like Y).
4. 1-star about food quality.
5. 1-star about service or wait time.
6. Suspected fake or never-visited review.

Rules:
- Warm, specific, plainspoken. No chain-restaurant corporate voice.
- Bad-review templates: apologize once, invite offline, no arguing about taste.
- Leave [brackets] where I insert the reviewer's detail.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Never argue about taste in public. "Our pizza is authentic" against "pizza was bland" helps no one.
- Do not get defensive about health or cleanliness claims. Address calmly, move offline fast.
- Identical replies across Yelp and Google get noticed. Vary the wording per platform.
- Comping a meal publicly in every reply trains readers to complain for free food. Offer it privately.
