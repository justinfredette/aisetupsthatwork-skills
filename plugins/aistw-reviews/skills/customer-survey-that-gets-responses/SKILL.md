# Write a Short Customer Survey People Actually Finish

## When to use

Small business owners who want customer feedback but get single-digit response rates.

## Steps

1. Write down the 3 to 5 things you actually need to learn. Everything else is cut.
2. Ask AI to draft the survey: most important questions first, never demographic questions up front, no non-essential required fields.
3. Apply the 3S rule from Surva: every question Simple, Specific, and Short. Plain words, short sentences.
4. Add branching so people skip irrelevant questions. Keep the whole thing under 5 minutes.
5. Write a personalized invitation: their name, a reference to their recent purchase, sent from a real person. Surva reports personalized invites improve response 7 to 15 percent over generic blasts.
6. If you offer an incentive, state it at the start and match it to your audience. Surva reports incentives lift response 10 to 15 percent or more.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me write a short customer survey. Plain words, no hype.

Business: [what you do]
What I need to learn: [3-5 things, e.g. why they chose us, what almost stopped them]
Audience: [who gets it]

Write:
1. 8-12 survey questions, most important first. No demographic questions up front. Mark any question that should not be required.
2. Apply the 3S rule: simple, specific, short. No matrix questions.
3. A short invitation email: personalized, from a real person, referencing their recent purchase. Under 80 words.
4. If I offer an incentive: one line stating it at the start.

Rules:
- Every question must earn its place. Cut anything I do not need.
- Show me the draft first. I will review before sending anything.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Asking everything you are curious about. Every extra question cuts completions.
- Leading questions that fish for praise. You get flattery, not data.
- Incentives attract reward-chasers if overused. Match the incentive to real customers.
- Forgetting the invitation. A great survey with a generic blast still gets ignored.
