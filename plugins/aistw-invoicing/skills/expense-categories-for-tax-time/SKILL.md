# Sort your expenses into tax-ready categories

## When to use

A sole proprietor staring at a shoebox of receipts in March

## Steps

1. Gather your expenses for the year: bank exports, a spreadsheet, or a plain list.
2. Paste the prompt below along with your expense list.
3. The AI sorts each expense into Schedule C categories like advertising, supplies, travel, and meals.
4. Review the uncertain ones. Mixed personal and business purchases need your judgment.
5. Ask the AI to total each category. These totals go straight onto your tax forms.
6. Bring the categorized list to your accountant. You just saved them hours, which saves you money.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Categorize my business expenses for taxes. I am a sole proprietor filing Schedule C. Here is my expense list: [PASTE LIST WITH AMOUNTS]. Sort each expense into the right IRS Schedule C category (advertising, car and truck, contract labor, insurance, office expenses, rent, supplies, travel, meals, utilities, other). Flag anything that might be only partly deductible, like meals or mixed-use items. Then total each category. If you are unsure about an item, say so instead of guessing.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI confidently categorizes something it should have flagged. Gray areas need your accountant, not a chatbot.
- Personal expenses mixed into the list. Separate them before you start, or the totals are wrong.
- Forgetting the home office. If you have a dedicated workspace, ask about the simplified method.
- Treating the AI output as tax advice. It is a sorting assistant. Your accountant makes the calls.
