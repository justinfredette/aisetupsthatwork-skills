# Build an invoice template that gets paid faster

## When to use

A small business owner still sending plain-text invoices or Word docs with no payment link

## Steps

1. List what goes on your invoice today, or admit you are starting from scratch.
2. Paste the prompt below with your business details.
3. The AI gives you a field-by-field layout: what goes where and why.
4. Add your logo and brand colors. Invoices with a logo get paid faster.
5. Put the due date near the top, in plain words like "Due March 15", not buried at the bottom.
6. Add a payment link or button. Clients who can pay in two clicks pay sooner.
7. Send the first invoice the day the work is done. Every day of delay adds a day to your wait.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Design an invoice template for my business. Business name: [NAME]. I do [TYPE OF WORK]. My clients pay by [check / bank transfer / card / payment link]. Give me a field-by-field layout: header, bill-to section, line items, totals, payment terms, and payment instructions. For payment terms, use plain words like "Due in 14 days" instead of jargon. Include a notes section under 50 words. Tell me the three highest-impact changes vs a basic Word invoice.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Burying the due date in small print at the bottom. Put it near the total where eyes land.
- No payment link, just bank details. Every extra step loses you days.
- Forgetting the invoice number or date. You need both for your own records.
- Sending a PDF with no reminder setup. Pair the template with automated reminders.
