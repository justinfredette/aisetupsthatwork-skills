# Build your year-end tax document checklist

## When to use

A small business owner who scrambles for documents every March

## Steps

1. Tell the AI your business structure, whether you have employees or contractors, and how you track books.
2. Paste the prompt below. The AI builds your personalized document checklist.
3. Start collecting in December, not March. Aim to have 80 percent gathered by year end.
4. Check items off as you go. Paste your progress back to the AI and it tells you what is missing.
5. Prioritize the time-sensitive ones: 1099s to contractors are due January 31.
6. Hand the organized package to your accountant. Organized clients get faster, cheaper returns.
7. This is not tax advice, and a chatbot cannot be your tax preparer. Do not paste your SSN, account numbers, or full tax forms into it. Anything it tells you must be checked against IRS sources or a licensed professional.
8. Use the AI as an organizer and explainer, not a filer: it can summarize what a form is for, draft questions for your preparer, and sort your document list. It gets less reliable on complex situations and recent law changes, so ask it to name the IRS publication for each claim and open that publication yourself.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Build my year-end tax document checklist. I am a [sole proprietor / single-member LLC / S-corp / partnership]. I have [employees / contractors / neither]. I track my books in [QuickBooks / spreadsheet / shoebox]. List every document I should gather before filing: income records, expense records, payroll forms, asset purchases, mileage, home office, prior year return. Mark which ones have hard deadlines and what those deadlines are. Then I will tell you what I have, and you tell me what is still missing.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Missing contractor W-9s in January. You cannot file 1099s without them. Collect them when you hire, not at year end.
- Forgetting digital income: Stripe, PayPal, and platform payouts all count and all get reported.
- December bank statements arriving late. Download them yourself in early January instead of waiting.
- Treating the checklist as tax advice. It is an organizer. Your accountant decides what goes on the return.
- Trusting a confident-sounding AI answer on a deduction. Experts say AI can invent deductions or state outdated law with full confidence, and you, not the chatbot, pay the penalty.
