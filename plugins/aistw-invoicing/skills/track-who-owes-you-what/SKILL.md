# Track who owes you what, every week

## When to use

An office manager or freelancer who sends invoices and discovers overdue ones by accident

## Steps

1. Export or list your unpaid invoices: client name, invoice number, amount, invoice date, due date.
2. Paste the prompt below with your list.
3. The AI sorts invoices into buckets: current, 1 to 30 days late, 31 to 60, 61 to 90, over 90.
4. It flags the high-risk ones: big amounts, very late, or repeat late payers.
5. You get a contact list for the week: who to email, who to call, in what order.
6. Work the list. Update it when payments land.
7. Repeat weekly. Ten minutes a week beats a quarterly panic.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
I need a weekly review of my unpaid invoices. Here is the list (client, invoice number, amount, invoice date, due date): [PASTE LIST]. Sort them into aging buckets: current, 1-30 days overdue, 31-60, 61-90, over 90. Flag the high-risk ones: large amounts, very overdue, or clients who are often late. Then give me this week's contact list in priority order, with a suggested action for each: email reminder, personal email, or phone call. Total each bucket.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The list is out of date because nobody updates it when payments arrive. Garbage in, garbage out.
- Treating all overdue invoices the same. A $200 invoice 10 days late and a $5,000 invoice 80 days late need different actions.
- Never escalating. If the 90-day bucket keeps growing, your process needs teeth: late fees, paused work, or collections.
- Forgetting credit notes and partial payments. Net the amounts before sorting.
