# Write payment terms that actually protect you

## When to use

A freelancer or small business owner who has been burned by vague payment agreements

## Steps

1. Write down how you bill today: when you invoice, when you expect payment, what goes wrong.
2. Paste the prompt below with your details.
3. The AI drafts payment terms covering deposit, due date, late fees, and paused projects.
4. Read every line. Delete anything you would not enforce. Terms you will not enforce are worse than no terms.
5. Check late-fee rules for your state. Some states cap them or restrict surcharges.
6. Put the final terms on your quotes, your contracts, and your invoices. All three.
7. When a client pushes back, negotiate scope or timing, not the existence of terms.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Draft payment terms for my business. I am a [FREELANCER / CONTRACTOR / SERVICE BUSINESS] doing [TYPE OF WORK]. I bill [per project / hourly / monthly retainer]. My typical invoice is $[AMOUNT]. Write payment terms covering: 1) deposit required before work starts, 2) when the balance is due, 3) late fee for overdue payments, 4) what happens if the client pauses the project mid-way, 5) what happens if the client cancels. Keep the language plain and professional, no legalese. Note anywhere I should check local law.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI writes terms you would never enforce. If you will not charge the late fee, do not list it.
- Late-fee caps vary by state. What is fine in one state may be restricted in another. Check yours.
- Putting terms only in the contract and not on the invoice. The invoice is what the client actually reads.
- Treating AI-drafted terms as legal advice. For big contracts, get a real review.
