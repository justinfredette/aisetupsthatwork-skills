# Ask Better Retirement Questions With AI

## When to use

DIY planners who want to ask smarter questions about retirement. This is not financial advice; check with a registered investment professional before acting.

## Steps

1. Write down the three classic questions: how much should I save, when should I claim Social Security, and how much can I withdraw each year.
2. Pull your Social Security statement to see estimated benefits at different claiming ages.
3. Run the prompt below with your rough numbers. Use totals only, never account numbers.
4. Check every AI claim against primary sources like the Social Security Administration before believing it.
5. Take the organized question list to a registered investment professional for the actual plan.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me organize my retirement questions. You are organizing, NOT giving financial advice.

Age: [age]
Rough household income: [amount]
Rough retirement savings total: [amount]
Debts: [mortgage, loans: amounts only]
Planned retirement age: [age or "unsure"]

Rules:
- List the key questions I should be asking: savings rate, Social Security claiming age, withdrawal rate, health care costs, tax treatment.
- For Social Security timing, note only what the Social Security Administration states: benefits can be claimed between 62 and 70 and are higher the longer you wait, up to 70.
- Do NOT recommend investments, predict returns, or tell me what to do with my money.
- Flag anything that needs a professional with "[ask a registered investment professional]".
- End with: "Verify everything here against primary sources and talk to a professional before acting."

Review the output before you rely on any of it.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI cannot give financial advice and may invent figures, returns, or rules. Every number needs a primary-source check.
- Do not paste account numbers, logins, or full statements into a chatbot. Totals are enough for planning questions.
- Beware of anyone, human or AI, promising guaranteed investment returns. That is a classic fraud flag.
- Tax and Social Security rules change. Confirm current rules on ssa.gov rather than trusting a chat answer.
