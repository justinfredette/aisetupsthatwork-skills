# Set Up Contractor W-9s and 1099s Without the Scramble

## When to use

Small business owners who pay freelancers or contractors and dread tax season paperwork.

## Steps

1. Before paying any contractor, have them complete and sign Form W-9. The IRS says this is how you get their correct name and taxpayer ID number.
2. Store each W-9 securely. A signed W-9 you can rely on protects you from backup withholding, which the IRS sets at 24 percent of reportable payments.
3. Track every contractor payment in one spreadsheet through the year: name, amount, date, service.
4. In January, list everyone you paid $600 or more. The IRS requires Form 1099-NEC for payments at that threshold starting with tax year 2020.
5. File Form 1099-NEC by January 31. The IRS notes there is no automatic 30-day extension, though hardship extensions may exist.
6. Ask AI to draft your reminder emails and a filing checklist, then have your accountant or tax software confirm everything before filing.

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Missing the January 31 deadline. Mark it on your calendar in December, not January.
- Assuming your payment app handles it. Some do, some do not: verify, do not assume.
- Pasting real SSNs or EINs into a chatbot while drafting. Use placeholders, always.
- Treating AI output as tax advice. It is a drafting helper. Your accountant makes the calls.
