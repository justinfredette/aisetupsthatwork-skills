# Chase late payments without the awkward email

## When to use

A freelancer or small business owner with an overdue invoice who hates awkward money conversations

## Steps

1. Gather the facts: invoice number, amount owed, original due date, and how many days late it is.
2. Decide on the tone. First reminder: friendly nudge. Second: firm. Third: final warning.
3. Paste the prompt below into ChatGPT or Claude with your details filled in.
4. Read the draft carefully. Fix anything that does not sound like you.
5. Attach the original invoice to your email. People pay faster when they do not have to hunt for it.
6. Send it yourself from your own email. Never let the AI send anything on your behalf.
7. If there is no reply in a week, ask the AI to write the firmer second notice using the same facts.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Write a late-payment reminder email. Facts: my name is [YOUR NAME], my business is [BUSINESS NAME]. The client is [CLIENT NAME]. Invoice [INVOICE NUMBER] for $[AMOUNT] was due on [DUE DATE], which is [X] days ago. The work was [BRIEF DESCRIPTION]. Tone: [friendly nudge / firm / final notice]. Keep it under 150 words. Include a clear ask: pay by [DATE] or reply with a payment date. Do not invent a late fee unless I gave you one.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI invents a late fee or penalty you never agreed to. Check every number before sending.
- The tone comes out too aggressive for a first reminder. Say "friendly nudge" explicitly in the prompt.
- The draft uses the wrong amount or date. You typed them, but double-check anyway.
- Sending the email without the invoice attached. The draft cannot attach files for you.
