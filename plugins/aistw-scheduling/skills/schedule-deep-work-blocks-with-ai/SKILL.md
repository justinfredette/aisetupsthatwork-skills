# Schedule deep-work blocks that actually hold

## When to use

Knowledge workers whose important work keeps losing to meetings, messages, and "quick questions."

## Steps

1. Export or screenshot two typical weeks of your calendar. Note when meetings cluster.
2. Tell the chatbot your working hours, your peak energy window, and what counts as deep work for you.
3. Ask it to propose 2–3 weekly deep-work blocks of 60–90 minutes in your best windows, away from meeting clusters.
4. Put the blocks on your calendar as real events, marked busy, with the specific deliverable in the title.
5. Set a shutdown ritual: phone in another room, notifications off, one clear goal written down before the block starts.
6. Protect the blocks for two weeks, then review. Move them if the timing fights reality, but do not delete them.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Find my deep-work windows and protect them.

MY CALENDAR (two typical weeks, meetings and fixed commitments):
[paste or describe]

MY WORKING HOURS: [e.g. 9-5]
MY PEAK ENERGY: [e.g. 9-11am]
MY DEEP WORK: [e.g. writing proposals, coding, analysis (the work that needs full focus)]

Propose 2-3 recurring 60-90 minute deep-work blocks per week in my best windows, avoiding meeting clusters. For each block, suggest the specific deliverable format for the title (e.g. "Draft proposal section 2", not "work on project"). Also draft a 5-minute shutdown ritual I do before each block starts.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Scheduling deep work in your low-energy hours. A 3pm focus block after lunch meetings is decorative.
- Letting the block become optional. Treat it like a meeting with your most important client: you.
- No shutdown ritual. Five minutes of setup (close tabs, phone away, goal written) is what makes the block work.
