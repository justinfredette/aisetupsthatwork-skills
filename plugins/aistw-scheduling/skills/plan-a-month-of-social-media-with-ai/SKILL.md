# Plan a month of social media on one calendar

## When to use

Small business owners who post in bursts, go quiet for weeks, then feel guilty about the dead feed.

## Steps

1. Pick 4–5 content pillars: the themes you will rotate (tips, behind the scenes, customer proof, offers, questions).
2. Mark the month’s key dates: holidays, sales, events, seasonal moments.
3. Ask the chatbot to fill a month-long calendar: pillar, platform, and post idea per slot, 3–5 posts a week.
4. Batch the creation: one session for writing, one for photos or graphics. Do not alternate.
5. Schedule everything in your scheduler of choice, leaving 2–3 slots open for timely posts.
6. At month’s end, check what performed and adjust next month’s pillars. Ten minutes, not an analytics breakdown.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Build me a one-month social media content calendar.

BUSINESS: [what you do, who you serve, town/region]
PLATFORMS: [e.g. Facebook, Instagram]
POSTS PER WEEK PER PLATFORM: [e.g. 3]
CONTENT PILLARS: [e.g. tips, behind-the-scenes, customer reviews, offers]
KEY DATES THIS MONTH: [holidays, sales, events]
TONE: [plainspoken, no hype]

Fill a 4-week calendar. For each slot: date, platform, pillar, and a one-line post idea. Spread pillars evenly so no week is all promos. Leave 2-3 slots marked OPEN for timely posts. After the calendar, list what I should prepare in my batch creation session (photos needed, graphics, offers to confirm).
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Planning a month and then not batching the creation. The calendar without the batch session is just a wish list.
- Letting AI write the actual posts unchecked. It drafts ideas well. Final captions need your voice.
- Posting everywhere. Two platforms done well beat five done badly.
