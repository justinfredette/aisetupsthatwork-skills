# Draft the Hard Emails: Saying No and Following Up Twice

## When to use

A professional who dreads saying no to meetings and lets follow-ups die from awkwardness

## Steps

1. Decide the outcome before you open the chatbot: decline, decline with alternative, or follow up again. AI writes better when the decision is already made.
2. Tell the AI the relationship context: is this your boss, a peer, a client, or a vendor? Tone depends on power, not just words.
3. Paste the prompt below with your situation filled in. Ask for three versions: firm, warm, and brief.
4. Read all three drafts out loud. Pick the one that sounds closest to how you actually talk.
5. Rewrite it yourself. Change at least a few sentences. AI drafts sound fine but generic, and your own phrasing is what keeps relationships intact.
6. For a second follow-up with no reply, use the follow-up variant of the prompt. Two follow-ups is the honest limit before you let it go or pick up the phone.
7. Send it yourself. Never let AI send anything on your behalf without your review.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Draft an email for this situation: [DESCRIBE IT, e.g. declining a meeting invite from a vendor, saying no to a project request from a peer, following up a second time on an unanswered quote request]. The recipient is [BOSS, PEER, CLIENT, VENDOR]. Give me three versions: 1. FIRM: polite but leaves no doubt. 2. WARM: keeps the relationship, offers an alternative like notes after the meeting or a future time. 3. BRIEF: under 50 words. Keep every version under 150 words. No corporate jargon.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI writes something too soft and the person does not realize you said no. Read your draft and check: could a busy person misunderstand the answer? If yes, firm it up.
- The AI invents details you never agreed to, like a call time or a deliverable. Verify every commitment in the draft before sending.
- Tone mismatch. What reads as 'warm' to AI can read as stiff or sarcastic at work. Read it out loud in your own voice.
- AI cannot read office politics. It does not know that declining your boss's boss's meeting needs a different approach than declining a vendor. You are the politics layer.
