# Write Cold Outreach That Does Not Sound Like Spam

## When to use

A small business owner who needs new clients but hates sending spam

## Steps

1. Build a small list: 10 to 15 prospects who genuinely fit what you do. Outreach is never a volume game; a small, managed list beats hundreds of blasts.
2. For each prospect, spend 5 minutes on their website, socials, or recent news. Note one specific, real observation: a service they added, a project they finished, a gap you can honestly comment on.
3. Paste your research notes into Claude with the prompt below and get a draft under 120 words.
4. Read the draft yourself. Check the observation is true, not something the AI invented to fill space.
5. Keep one plain ask: a 15-minute call or a simple reply. One ask, nothing else.
6. Send from your normal email address, not a marketing tool. Track who you contacted, when, and what they said.
7. One follow-up after a week, max. Then move on. Silence is an answer.
8. Ask AI to turn your prospect research notes into a short call script with [bracketed] spots for name, company, and the one detail you found. Tell it to keep the opener under 30 seconds: your name, one specific thing you noticed about their business, one question. Show output first, read it out loud, and fix anything that sounds stiff before you dial.
9. Open every call with a permission line: 'I know I'm calling out of the blue - do you have 30 seconds so I can tell you why I'm calling?' Then stop talking. If they say no, ask when a better time is and hang up politely. Respecting the no is what keeps the call from feeling like spam.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
You are helping me write cold outreach to win new clients. My business: [DESCRIBE WHAT YOU DO AND WHO YOU SERVE, e.g. "commercial cleaning for dental offices in Cleveland"].

Here is my research on one prospect:
[PASTE PROSPECT WEBSITE TEXT, LINKEDIN NOTES, OR A SHORT BULLET LIST OF FACTS]

Write a cold email under 120 words. Rules:
- Open with ONE specific observation about their business from my research, not a compliment about their "impressive website"
- Say what I do in one sentence
- One plain ask (a 15-minute call or a reply), nothing else
- No hype, no fake warmth, no "I would love to pick your brain"
- Sign off simply

If my research is too thin to write a specific opening, say so instead of inventing one.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI invents a fake observation about the prospect. Verify every specific claim against your research before sending.
- Fake warmth kills trust. "Hey bestie, I LOVE your content" to a stranger reads as manipulation, not friendliness.
- Sending to hundreds of people at once. Keep the list small and track responses, or the replies will rot in your inbox.
- No unsubscribe option on cold commercial email can violate CAN-SPAM rules in the US. One short line at the bottom ("Reply STOP to opt out") keeps you safe.
- Reading the script word for word makes you sound like a telemarketer. Work from bullet points and talk like a person, not a recording.
