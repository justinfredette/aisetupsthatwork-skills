# Build a Template Bank for Repeat Business Emails

## When to use

A plumber, contractor, or freelancer who sends the same quote follow-ups and payment nudges every week

## Steps

1. List the emails you send repeatedly: quote follow-ups, late-payment nudges, booked-out replies, review requests, thank-you notes.
2. Ask ChatGPT to draft one template for each, using the prompt below. Keep every template under 120 words.
3. Strip anything that sounds like a greeting card. Plain and friendly beats warm and wordy.
4. Save each template with [BRACKET] placeholders for name, amount, date, and invoice number.
5. Store them in Gmail templates (Settings, Advanced, Templates) or a text expander so you can insert them with a shortcut.
6. Before each send, fill in the brackets and add one personal line about the actual job. That one line is what makes it feel real.
7. List your 10-15 most repeated support questions and group them into four buckets: acknowledge (ticket received), update (still working on it), resolve (fixed - here is what to check), and follow up (did it stick).
8. Ask AI to draft one short reply per bucket, with [bracketed] spots for name, ticket number, dates, and next steps. Show output first. Keep each reply to 3-4 sentences in your own tone, and store them where you already answer messages: saved replies in your helpdesk, a text expander, or a shared doc.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Act as a writing assistant for a small business owner. I send the same types of emails over and over. Write me a template for each email listed below.

Rules for every template:
- Under 120 words
- Friendly but plain, no fluff
- Leave placeholders in [BRACKETS] for anything I must fill in (name, amount, date, invoice number)
- End with one clear next step

My emails:
1. Follow-up on a quote I sent a week ago
2. Nudge about an overdue invoice
3. "We are booked out for [TIMEFRAME], next opening is [DATE]"
4. Request for a review after the job is done
5. Thank-you after payment received

Write a one-line subject line for each. Ask me what my business is and how I sign my emails before you start.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI writes too formally. Tell it to write like you text a customer, then edit in your own voice.
- The AI invents specifics like a fake invoice number or date. Only real details you typed yourself go in the brackets.
- A template with [CLIENT] in it goes out unfilled. Read it once before sending, every time.
- The AI softens deadlines and firm asks. Check that the due date and the payment link survived the draft.
- A reply that goes out with [NAME] still in brackets breaks trust. Read every reply once before sending, every time.
