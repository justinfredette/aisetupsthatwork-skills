# Triage Your Newsletters Once a Week

## When to use

A newsletter subscriber who keeps falling behind and feels guilty about the unread pile

## Steps

1. Create a label or folder called 'Newsletters' in your email. Then create a filter so anything with an unsubscribe link or from your newsletter senders skips the inbox and lands there.
2. Let the folder fill up during the week. Do not open it until your scheduled session.
3. Pick a fixed weekly slot, 15 minutes, for example Sunday morning or Friday afternoon. Put it on your calendar.
4. Open the folder and scan the subject lines. Copy the list of subject lines and senders into Claude or ChatGPT with the prompt below.
5. The AI scores each one against your interests. Open the high scorers, skim them, then archive or delete everything in the folder.
6. If a newsletter scores low three weeks in a row, unsubscribe. The point of this system is that keeping it around has a cost.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
I get these newsletters this week. My interests are [LIST YOUR INTERESTS, e.g. Vermont news, AI tools, running]. Score each newsletter 1 to 5 on whether it is worth my time to open, and give a one-line reason for each score. Newsletters: [PASTE SUBJECT LINES AND SENDERS]. Reply as a simple list, highest score first.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The filter catches too much. Some real emails contain unsubscribe links too. Check the folder after setting up the filter and whitelist anything personal.
- You skip the weekly session. The folder becomes a second inbox. Keep the session short and fixed on the calendar so it sticks.
- The AI scores everything highly. Make your interests list specific, not generic, so the scores actually discriminate.
- You hoard 'read later' newsletters. 'Read later' means never. Open, skim, delete, done.
