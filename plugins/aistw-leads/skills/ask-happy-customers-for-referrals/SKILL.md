# Ask happy customers for referrals

## When to use

Small service businesses that get most work by word of mouth but never actually ask for it.

## Steps

1. Pick finished jobs from the last 30 to 90 days where the customer was clearly pleased.
2. For each one, note the customer name, what you did, and one specific detail (the room, the problem fixed, the result).
3. Paste the details plus the prompt below into the AI.
4. Read the draft. Make sure it sounds like you, not a marketing department.
5. Send it yourself, ideally within a week of the job wrapping up.
6. When a referral comes in, thank the referrer. A short thank-you note keeps the loop going.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Write a short referral request email. Under 100 words. Warm and direct, like a real person wrote it. No hype, no exclamation marks.

My business: [BUSINESS NAME]: [WHAT YOU DO, e.g. "residential cleaning in Rutland"]
Customer: [NAME]
What I did for them: [ONE SENTENCE, e.g. "deep-cleaned their whole house before they listed it"]
One specific detail: [DETAIL, e.g. "the kitchen took three hours but the stove looks new"]

The email should:
- Thank them briefly and mention the specific detail so it feels personal.
- Say I have room for [1-2] more customers like them right now.
- Ask if they know anyone who needs [SERVICE] and would appreciate an introduction.
- Make it easy: they can just reply with a name, or forward my contact info.

Sign as [YOUR NAME].
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Asking before the customer is actually happy. Wait until the job is done and they have said something positive.
- Sending the same generic email to everyone. One specific detail per customer is what makes it work.
- Never following up the thank-you. When a referral lands, tell the referrer. People refer again when they feel appreciated.
