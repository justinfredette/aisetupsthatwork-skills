# Sort trade show leads before they go cold

## When to use

Small businesses that exhibit at trade shows and home shows, then let the lead pile sit for weeks.

## Steps

1. The night the show ends, photograph every card and note page so nothing gets lost.
2. Sort every lead into three tiers: hot (specific need, timeline, or asked for a price), warm (right kind of business, interested, no urgency), cold (students, suppliers pitching you, brochure collectors).
3. For hot and warm leads, write one sentence each: what they asked about and what you promised.
4. Paste the sorted list plus the prompt below into the AI.
5. Message every hot lead personally within 3 days. Reference the actual conversation, not the show.
6. Send warm leads the short sequence: what you promised, then one useful addition a week later.
7. File the cold tier with no active follow-up. They cost you nothing to keep and nothing to chase.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Build a trade show follow-up plan from my sorted leads. Direct, human tone. No generic "great meeting you at the show" lines.

Show: [SHOW NAME], [DATES]
My business: [WHAT YOU DO]

HOT leads (message within 3 days, personal):
1. [NAME, COMPANY]: asked about [SPECIFIC THING]. I promised [WHAT I SAID I WOULD SEND].
[ADD MORE]

WARM leads (short sequence):
1. [NAME, COMPANY]: [WHAT THEY WERE INTERESTED IN]. No specific promise made.
[ADD MORE]

For each HOT lead, draft a short first message: reference the specific thing they asked about, deliver or promise the item, suggest one easy next step (a call, a sample, a site visit).
For each WARM lead, draft two messages: message one delivers something useful related to their interest; message two (a week later) adds one new detail and one easy next step.
Keep every message under 120 words. Sign as [YOUR NAME], [BUSINESS], [PHONE].
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Waiting two weeks to follow up. After a week they do not remember your face, and your message reads as spam.
- Blasting the whole list with one generic email. Hot leads can tell they got the mail merge.
- No notes on the cards. "Met at the show" is not a note. Write the one sentence while they are still standing there.
