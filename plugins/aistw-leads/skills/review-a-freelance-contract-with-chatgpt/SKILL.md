# Review a Freelance Contract with ChatGPT or Claude

## When to use

For freelancers and solo business owners who get a contract from a client and want to understand it before signing, without paying for a full legal review every time.

## Steps

1. Write down what you agreed to with the client: scope, price, payment schedule, deadlines.
2. Black out anything you don't want to share, like bank details. Upload the contract with the prompt below.
3. Read the plain-English summary of each section and check it matches what you agreed.
4. Look at the flags: when you get paid, who owns the work and when, how the contract can end, how long any non-compete or confidentiality lasts, and who pays if something goes wrong.
5. Ask for polite questions or suggested edits for the clauses you want changed, and send them to the client yourself.
6. For high-value work or anything you don't understand after this, have a lawyer read it. USA.gov lists free and low-cost legal aid.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me review a contract before I sign it. I'm a freelancer. Don't contact anyone for me.

Attached: the contract.
What I agreed to: [scope, price, payment schedule, deadlines]

1. Summarize each section in one or two plain-English sentences.
2. Flag anything that doesn't match what I agreed to.
3. Flag clauses worth questioning: payment timing and late payment, who owns the work and when ownership passes, how either side can end the contract, non-compete or non-solicit terms, confidentiality length, liability and indemnity, and unlimited revisions. Say why for each, quoting the clause.
4. For each flag, write a polite question or a suggested edit I can send the client.

Rules: Quote the contract exactly. Don't invent clauses or laws. If something depends on my state's law, say so and tell me to check. This isn't legal advice.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- It's not a lawyer and can miss terms that are normal or risky in your industry or state.
- It reads only what you upload. If the contract points to another document, like terms on a website, upload that too.
- Long scanned contracts can be misread. Check every quoted clause against the original.
