# Follow up on quotes that went cold

## When to use

Service businesses that send quotes and then never hear back, and hate the awkwardness of chasing.

## Steps

1. List the quotes that are 2 or more days old with no reply. One row per quote: name, date sent, amount, what it covered.
2. For each one, paste the details plus the prompt below into the AI.
3. Get four drafts back: a day 2 check that the quote arrived, a day 7 nudge with one useful thing, a day 14 last check, and a day 21 message closing the file.
4. Personalize each draft with one real detail from your conversation with that customer.
5. Send the first follow-up yourself. Set reminders for the next three, not auto-sends.
6. Stop as soon as the customer replies, books, says no, or asks you to stop.
7. If the day 21 message gets no reply, close the quote and free up the calendar slot.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Write four short follow-up emails for a quote that got no reply. Keep each under 120 words. Plain, friendly tone. No pushy sales language.

Quote details:
- Customer: [NAME]
- Sent on: [DATE]
- Amount: [AMOUNT]
- What it covered: [SCOPE]
- Something useful I can add: [PHOTO / TIMELINE / WARRANTY NOTE / CASE DETAIL]

Email 1 (day 2): Confirm they received the quote and ask if anything needs clarifying. Low pressure.
Email 2 (day 7): Re-enter with the useful thing I listed above. Give them a real reason to reply.
Email 3 (day 14): Last check. Offer to check back later if the timing is wrong.
Email 4 (day 21): Short closing email. Say I'm closing the quote on my end and they can reach out anytime. Leave the door open.

Subject lines for each. Sign as [YOUR NAME], [BUSINESS].
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Following up on a quote the customer already answered. Check your inbox and spam before each send.
- Sending all four emails on a rigid schedule when the customer already opened the quote six times. If they are engaging, call instead.
- Copy-pasting the AI draft without adding the one personal detail. Generic follow-ups read as generic.
- If email 2 mentions a photo or file, attach it before sending.
