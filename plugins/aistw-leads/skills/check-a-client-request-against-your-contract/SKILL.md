# Check a Client Request Against Your Contract

## When to use

Freelancers and small shops getting "one more thing" requests. This is a judgment helper, not legal advice about your contract.

## Steps

1. Get the request in writing. If it came by phone, send a quick email confirming what was asked.
2. Paste the scope section of your contract and the new request into the chat with the prompt below.
3. Read the AI’s sorting: covered by the contract, gray area, or new work. Check it against your own reading.
4. If it is your mistake, treat it as a correction, not a paid extra.
5. If it is new work, estimate the extra effort, then agree on price and schedule with the client before starting.
6. Confirm the decision in a short email: what was decided, who does what, and by when. That email becomes the record.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me check whether a client request is in scope. You are comparing text, NOT giving legal advice.

My contract scope section:
[Paste the scope, deliverables, revision limits, and timeline]

Client’s new request:
[Paste the request exactly as written]

Rules:
- Sort the request into one of three buckets: clearly covered, gray area, or new work. Quote the contract lines that support your sorting.
- If gray area, list what is ambiguous and what I should ask the client to clarify.
- If new work, draft a short reply: acknowledge the request, quote the added fee and timeline shift, and ask for written approval before I start.
- If it looks like fixing my own mistake, say so plainly and suggest treating it as a correction.
- End with: "You make the final call. This is a reading aid, not legal advice."

Review the sorting before you act on it.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI reads text; it does not know your verbal agreements or relationship history. Your judgment rules.
- Gray areas need a clarifying question to the client, not a guess. Ask them to point to the requirement.
- Do not paste an entire client contract with sensitive commercial terms into a chatbot if you can avoid it. The scope section is usually enough.
- Agree on price and schedule before starting added work, not after.
