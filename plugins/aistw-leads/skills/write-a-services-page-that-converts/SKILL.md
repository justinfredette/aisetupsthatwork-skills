# Write a services page that converts

## When to use

Small business owners whose website lists services like a menu and gets inquiries from people who are not a fit.

## Steps

1. Write down each service in one plain sentence: what it is and who it is for.
2. List the questions every prospect asks before hiring you. These become your FAQ and your objection handling.
3. Gather proof: testimonials with names, before/after details, years in business, number of jobs done.
4. Paste everything plus the prompt below into the AI.
5. Read the draft and cut anything that sounds like every competitor. Keep what sounds like you.
6. Check the call to action on every service block. Each one should tell the reader exactly what to do next.
7. Publish one page per main service if you can, not one long page for everything.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Draft a services page for my small business website. Plainspoken, confident tone. Write for the customer, not about me.

Business: [BUSINESS NAME]: [WHAT YOU DO, WHERE]
Services (one line each):
1. [SERVICE]: [WHO IT IS FOR]
2. [SERVICE]: [WHO IT IS FOR]
3. [SERVICE]: [WHO IT IS FOR]

Proof I can use (only use what is listed here, do not invent any):
- [TESTIMONIAL WITH NAME, or "X years in business", or "X jobs completed"]
- [SECOND PROOF ITEM]

Questions prospects always ask:
- [QUESTION 1]
- [QUESTION 2]
- [QUESTION 3]

Structure the page:
1. Headline: say what I do and who it is for in one line. No clever wordplay.
2. For each service: what it is, who it fits, the outcome the customer gets (benefit, not feature), and a call to action ("Get a quote", "Book a walkthrough").
3. A short proof section using only my proof items above.
4. An FAQ answering the prospect questions in two sentences each.
5. One final call to action.

Rules: benefits over features. Short paragraphs. Every claim must come from my inputs above.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI invents testimonials or statistics. The prompt forbids this, but check every proof claim against your real inputs.
- A page that describes the business instead of the customer. If every paragraph starts with "we", rewrite it to start with "you".
- No call to action, or a weak one. "Contact us" is weak. "Get a quote in 24 hours" tells the reader what happens next.
