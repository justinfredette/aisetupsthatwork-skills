# Build a freelance portfolio with no clients

## When to use

New freelancers stuck in the no-clients-no-portfolio loop who need something to show this week.

## Steps

1. Pick your offer in one sentence. "I write email sequences for Shopify stores" gives you something to make samples for; "I do marketing" does not.
2. Invent two or three dream clients: a real type of business, a real-sounding name, a real problem. Example: a fake Instagram carousel for a neighborhood café, a blog post for a fictional wellness brand.
3. Make the samples as if they were paid work. Same effort, same finish. These are the only proof you have, so they have to be good.
4. Label every piece honestly. Write "This is a sample project created to show my skills." Do not imply it was client work; getting caught lying ends the career before it starts.
5. Add one case-study line per piece: the problem, what you made, what it would change for the business. Three sentences, no more.
6. Keep it tight: two or three strong pieces beat eight uneven ones. Cut anything you would not defend in a sales call.
7. Put it where clients look and start pitching. After the first real client, ask for a short testimonial and a referral, and swap the weakest sample out.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me build a freelance portfolio from zero. I have no clients yet.

My offer: [e.g. "I write product descriptions for Etsy sellers"]
My skills: [e.g. "short punchy copy, basic SEO, fast turnaround"]

Give me:
1. Three sample project briefs "as if" for realistic clients: business type, business name (fictional), the problem, and exactly what I should produce.
2. For each brief: what "done well" looks like, so I can judge my own work.
3. A one-line case-study template I can fill in per piece: problem, what I made, what it would change.
4. Five honest label wordings for marking these as sample work.

Rules:
- Keep every brief makeable in one afternoon by a beginner.
- No advice that involves lying about client work. Everything gets labeled as a sample.

Review the briefs before you start making. Pick the two you are most excited about and cut the third.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Presenting samples as client work. Label everything. The freelance world is small and getting caught is career-ending.
- Endless polishing instead of pitching. Two good samples plus ten pitches beats five perfect samples plus zero pitches.
- Samples for five different services. Pick one offer. A scattered portfolio tells clients you do nothing well.
