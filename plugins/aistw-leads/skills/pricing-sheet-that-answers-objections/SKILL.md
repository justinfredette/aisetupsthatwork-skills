# Build a pricing sheet that answers objections

## When to use

Service businesses tired of the "that is too expensive" conversation happening with zero context.

## Steps

1. Write down your packages or typical price ranges. Three tiers works well: essential, standard, premium.
2. List every price objection you heard in the last 90 days, in the customer words.
3. Next to each objection, write your honest one or two sentence answer.
4. Paste the packages, objections, and your answers plus the prompt below into the AI.
5. Read the draft. Every answer must sound like you on a good day, not a script.
6. Format it as one clean page: packages on top, "common questions" below.
7. Send it with quotes and keep a copy on your phone for in-person conversations.
8. Decide the new price, the start date, and whether it applies to current clients or only new ones. Paste those three facts plus one plain reason into the prompt. Never let the AI invent the numbers.
9. Ask for a short notice in your normal voice: name the old price and new price, the effective date, and the reason, then invite questions. Read it out loud before sending. If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Build a one-page pricing sheet for my service business. Calm, confident tone. No discount language unless I include it.

Business: [BUSINESS NAME]
Packages:
1. [PACKAGE NAME]: [PRICE OR RANGE], includes: [LIST]
2. [PACKAGE NAME]: [PRICE OR RANGE], includes: [LIST]
3. [PACKAGE NAME]: [PRICE OR RANGE], includes: [LIST]

What is NOT included (say it plainly): [LIST]

The objections I hear, with my real answers:
1. "[OBJECTION, e.g. That is more than I expected]" → My answer: [YOUR HONEST ANSWER]
2. "[OBJECTION]" → My answer: [YOUR HONEST ANSWER]
3. "[OBJECTION]" → My answer: [YOUR HONEST ANSWER]
[ADD MORE AS NEEDED]

Build the sheet:
- Top: the three packages, scannable, with what is included in each.
- Middle: a short "what affects the price" note (size, condition, timeline) so shoppers understand the range.
- Bottom: "Common questions": each objection as the question, my answer rewritten smoothly but keeping my meaning.
- Close with one line on next steps: [e.g. "Reply to this email and I will hold your slot for 14 days."]

Rules: do not invent discounts, guarantees, or services I did not list. Keep my answers honest, not salesy.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Hiding the price to "get them on the phone." Shoppers just move on to the competitor who shows theirs.
- Answering objections with defensiveness. The sheet should sound like you explaining, not arguing.
- Letting the AI invent a guarantee or discount. Check every line against what you actually offer.
- Burying the increase at the bottom of a long email. Put the new price, the date, and the reason up front, then stop.
