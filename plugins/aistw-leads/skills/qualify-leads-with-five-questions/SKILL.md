# Qualify leads with five questions

## When to use

Solo operators and small shops that waste hours quoting tire-kickers who were never going to buy.

## Steps

1. Write down your five questions once: 1) What problem are they solving? 2) What is the budget range? 3) Who decides? 4) When do they need it? 5) Are they in your service area and service list?
2. When a lead comes in, gather what you know: their initial message and any call notes.
3. Paste the lead details plus the prompt below into the AI.
4. Read the verdict: qualified, needs one more question, or not a fit.
5. Qualified leads get a quote. "Needs one more question" gets a short reply asking exactly that. Not-a-fit gets a polite no.
6. Track which questions catch the most bad fits, and sharpen them over time.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Qualify this lead for my business. Be blunt. I would rather hear "not a fit" now than waste a quote.

My business: [WHAT YOU DO, e.g. "residential landscaping in Rutland County"]
My service area: [AREA]
Jobs I do NOT take: [LIST, e.g. "commercial properties, tree removal over 20 feet"]
My typical job range: [PRICE RANGE, e.g. "$500 to $5,000"]

Lead details:
[PASTE THEIR MESSAGE AND ANY CALL NOTES]

Score the lead on these five questions (yes / no / unknown):
1. Need: Do they have a real problem I solve?
2. Budget: Is there any sign they can pay my typical range?
3. Decision-maker: Am I talking to the person who decides?
4. Timeline: Do they need this in a reasonable window?
5. Fit: Are they in my area and asking for work I actually do?

Then give me a verdict: QUALIFIED, ASK ONE MORE QUESTION (tell me exactly what to ask), or NOT A FIT (tell me why, and draft a polite two-sentence decline).
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Skipping qualification because the phone is quiet. A bad job costs more than no job.
- Letting the AI be too generous. Tell it to be blunt in the prompt or it will qualify everyone.
- Asking all five questions like an interrogation. Weave them into a normal conversation instead.
