# Turn a finished job into a case study

## When to use

Service businesses that do great work but have nothing on their website proving it.

## Steps

1. Pick your best recent job: clear problem, clear result, happy customer.
2. Ask the customer for permission to write it up. Offer to let them approve the draft.
3. Write down the raw facts: the situation before, what you did step by step, what changed after.
4. If you have numbers, confirm them with the customer. Never estimate a result.
5. Paste the facts plus the prompt below into the AI.
6. Send the draft to the customer for approval before publishing. Change anything they flag.
7. Publish it on your site and link it from the relevant services page.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Write a short case study for my website from these job facts. Plain language, no hype. Keep it under 400 words.

Business: [BUSINESS NAME]
Customer: [NAME or "a homeowner in [TOWN]" if anonymized]

The situation before (what was wrong):
[FACTS]

What I did (the approach, in plain terms):
[FACTS]

The result (only what I list here: do not invent numbers):
[FACTS, e.g. "drainage fixed in two days, basement dry through three heavy rains since"]

Customer quote (their exact words, or write "[QUOTE TO COME]" if I have not collected one yet):
[QUOTE OR PLACEHOLDER]

Structure:
1. Title: the outcome plus the customer type, e.g. "Dry basement for a [TOWN] homeowner".
2. One-paragraph summary: who, what was wrong, what changed.
3. The challenge: 2-4 bullets, specific and honest.
4. What I did: short paragraphs, decisions not jargon.
5. The result: the facts above, nothing more.
6. The customer quote.

Rules: every claim must come from my facts. If a number is not in my facts, do not include a number.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Invented results. The AI will happily write "saved the customer thousands" if you let it. The prompt blocks this, but verify every number.
- Publishing without customer approval. Always let them read it first. One surprised customer can cost you the relationship.
- Writing about yourself instead of the problem. The customer is the hero. You are the guide who fixed it.
