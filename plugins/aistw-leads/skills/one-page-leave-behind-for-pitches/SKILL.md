# Make a one-page leave-behind for pitches

## When to use

Solo operators and freelancers who pitch in person and want something to stay behind when they leave.

## Steps

1. Before the meeting, write down the client-specific problem in one sentence.
2. Note your recommended approach in one sentence, plus up to three workstreams.
3. Pick your single strongest proof point: a result, a testimonial, a relevant past job.
4. Write your commercial snapshot: scope, fee basis, timeline, key assumption.
5. Paste it all plus the prompt below into the AI.
6. Lay the draft out on one page: headline, three to five labeled blocks, one decision block at the bottom.
7. Print a few copies or save as PDF. Bring it to the meeting and leave it behind.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Draft a one-page leave-behind for my pitch. Scannable, plain language. One decision story, not a brochure.

Client: [CLIENT NAME / TYPE]
Their problem in one sentence: [PROBLEM]
My recommended approach in one sentence: [APPROACH]
Up to three workstreams: [1], [2], [3]
My proof (one compact example with real context): [PROOF: do not invent any part of it]
Commercial snapshot: scope [SCOPE], fee basis [e.g. fixed fee / per visit], timeline [TIMELINE], key assumption [ASSUMPTION]
Next step I want: [e.g. "approve the proposal by Friday"]

Structure the page:
1. Headline: the client-specific outcome, not my company name.
2. Current gap: one or two plain observations about their situation.
3. Recommended approach: the strategy plus the workstreams.
4. Proof: the compact example above.
5. Commercial snapshot: scope, fee, timeline, assumption: one line each.
6. Decision block: who decides, what action, by when.

Rules: keep it to one page. Every claim from my inputs only. The reader should be able to repeat the offer and the next step after one read.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Cramming twelve slides onto one page. One page means one story: opportunity, approach, proof, price, next step.
- Generic headlines. "About our services" says nothing. "Cut your turnover cleaning time in half" says everything.
- No proof, or invented proof. One real example beats a page of claims.
