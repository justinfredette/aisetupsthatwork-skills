# Win back past customers with email

## When to use

Service businesses with a list of old customers they have not talked to in months.

## Steps

1. Pull your customer list and mark anyone you have not served in 6 months or more.
2. Group them loosely: one-time jobs, repeat customers who stopped, seasonal customers due again.
3. For each group, note one true update: something new, something seasonal, or something you fixed.
4. Paste a group plus the prompt below into the AI. Get three short emails back.
5. Personalize the first line of each email with the customer name and their last job.
6. Send email one yourself. Wait a week between touches. Stop after three if there is no reply.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Write three short win-back emails for past customers. Each under 120 words. Warm, plain tone. No fake urgency, no exclamation marks.

My business: [BUSINESS NAME]: [WHAT YOU DO]
Customer group: [ONE-TIME / LAPSED REPEAT / SEASONAL]
What is true and new since they last used me: [ONE REAL UPDATE, e.g. "we now offer gutter cleaning with every roof job" or "it is leaf season again"]

Email 1: the check-in: Mention their last job by name if I give it. Ask how things are holding up. No offer yet, just re-open the door.
Email 2: the useful thing: Share the update above as something genuinely useful to them, not a sales pitch.
Email 3: the honest offer. One clear offer with a real reason (seasonal timing, a slot opening). Ask for a yes or no.

Subject lines for each. Sign as [YOUR NAME].
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Emailing people who never agreed to marketing emails. Past customers are safer than cold lists, but include an unsubscribe line anyway.
- Making up an offer to have something to say. Customers can tell. Use a real seasonal reason or skip the third email.
- Sending all three emails in one week. Space them out or you train people to ignore you.
