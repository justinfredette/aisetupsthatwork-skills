# Land Your First Upwork Client

## When to use

New freelancers with skills but no Upwork history, reviews, or earnings yet.

## Steps

1. Finish your profile to 100 percent: photo, overview, one employment history item, one skill. Upwork says complete profiles get better search visibility and trust.
2. Write your title with searchable keywords in the first 35 characters, since mobile cuts it off. Fill all 20 skill slots with terms clients search, like "Notion" or "Shopify".
3. Make the first 2 to 3 lines of your overview count. That is all search results show. State who you help and what you deliver.
4. Set your budget: proposals cost Connects at $0.15 each, 6 to 16 per proposal. Plan on $15 to $25 a month and 5 to 10 tailored proposals a day.
5. Target entry-level jobs posted within 24 hours with under 10 proposals. Write 150 to 250 words: hook with their specific problem in the first two lines, one proof link, two sentences of your plan, one clear next step.
6. Price 20 to 30 percent below market for the first job, not free. A small $30 task buys the 5-star review that unlocks bigger jobs.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me win my first Upwork client. Plain, practical, no hype.

My skill: [e.g. email marketing, Notion setup, bookkeeping]
Proof I have: [samples, past work, or relevant experience]
Rate I want: [hourly or per-project target]

Write:
1. A profile title under 70 characters with searchable keywords in the first 35.
2. A profile overview where the first 3 lines sell the outcome. No vague words like "hardworking".
3. A 150-250 word proposal template: hook with the client's specific problem in the first two lines, one proof link, two sentences of plan, one clear next step or question.
4. A checklist of what to customize per job.

Rules:
- Everything must sound like me, not a template.
- Remind me: keep all communication on Upwork, never share contact info in proposals.
- Show me the draft first. I will review before posting anything.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Scams are common for new freelancers. Red flags: unverified payment method, being asked to move to Telegram or WhatsApp, any fee you must pay, "free trial work", off-platform or crypto payment.
- Never share contact info in proposals. Upwork requires communication on-platform until a contract exists.
- Pricing at zero to "get reviews" attracts bad clients. Price 20 to 30 percent below market, not free.
- Copy-paste proposals get ignored. The first two lines must reference their specific problem.
