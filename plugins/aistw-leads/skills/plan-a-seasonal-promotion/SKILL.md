# Plan a seasonal promotion without the scramble

## When to use

Small businesses that do a holiday or seasonal push every year and always start too late.

## Steps

1. Pick one season and one clear offer. Fewer, clearer offers beat five confusing ones.
2. Give the AI your normal price, your cost per sale, and the discount you are considering. Ask it to check the math.
3. If the numbers do not work, change the offer, not your hopes. A smaller discount or a bundle often beats a deep cut.
4. Paste the confirmed offer plus the prompt below into the AI.
5. Get back: the announcement email, the launch-day email, the last-chance reminder, and a week of social posts.
6. Read everything. Fix anything that promises what you cannot deliver.
7. Schedule it all at least two weeks before the season starts, then focus on serving customers.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Plan my seasonal promotion. Practical tone. Everything must fit a one-person operation.

Business: [BUSINESS NAME]: [WHAT YOU SELL OR DO]
Season: [e.g. HOLIDAY 2026, SPRING CLEANUP SEASON]
Offer: [e.g. "15% off deep cleans booked in December" or "free gutter cleaning with every roof soft-wash in October"]
Offer runs: [START DATE] to [END DATE]
My normal price: [PRICE], my cost per sale: [COST]

First, check the math: at the discounted price, what do I earn per sale, and how many extra sales do I need to beat doing nothing? Show the numbers plainly. If the math is bad, say so and suggest a smaller discount or a bundle instead.

Then draft:
1. A "coming soon" email (under 150 words): tease the offer, give the start date.
2. A launch-day email (under 150 words): the offer, the deadline, one clear call to action.
3. A last-48-hours reminder (under 100 words): short, honest urgency, no fake countdown tricks.
4. Five social posts, one per day for launch week, each under 50 words, each with a different angle (the offer, the deadline, a customer result, a behind-the-scenes note, a FAQ answer).

Rules: never invent a discount I did not approve. Every claim must match my inputs.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Running the promotion the AI suggested without checking the math. Always verify the per-sale numbers yourself.
- Too many offers at once. One clear offer with a deadline beats a menu of discounts.
- Starting two weeks before the holiday. Most seasonal campaigns need a month of runway, more for Christmas.
