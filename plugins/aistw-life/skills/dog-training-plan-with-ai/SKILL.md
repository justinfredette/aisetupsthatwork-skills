# Build a Dog Training Plan With AI

## When to use

Dog owners who want a clear plan for one problem instead of random tips.

## Steps

1. Pick ONE behavior to train first. "Stop jumping" or "come when called", not both.
2. Note your dog's details: age, breed or size, what motivates them, when the problem happens.
3. Ask AI for a 2 to 4 week plan: one small step per stage, session structure, and what success looks like before moving on.
4. Train in short sessions and always end with play. The AKC's guidance: multiple short sessions beat one long one, and dogs cannot learn under stress.
5. Reward within seconds of the right behavior. A treat delivered late can reinforce the wrong thing.
6. If the dog gets it wrong, stay neutral: say "oops", show them the right move, and reward that. Teach what TO do instead of correcting what not to do.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Build a dog training plan for one specific behavior. Plain and practical.

Dog: [age, breed or size, e.g. 8-month lab mix]
Behavior to fix or teach: [one behavior, e.g. jumping on guests]
When it happens: [e.g. when someone walks in the door]
What motivates them: [e.g. cheese, tennis ball]

Write:
1. A 2-4 week plan, one small step per stage. Do not move on until the current step works.
2. Session structure: how long, how many reps, how to end.
3. Exactly what to reward and when, using positive reinforcement only.
4. Troubleshooting: the 3 most likely ways this goes wrong and what to do.

Rules:
- Positive reinforcement only. No punishment-based methods.
- Short sessions. End on a win, every time.
- If this behavior suggests fear or aggression, tell me to see a professional trainer instead.
- Show me the plan first. I will review before starting.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Training three behaviors at once. Pick one. Multitasking confuses the dog.
- Late rewards. More than a few seconds and the dog connects the treat to the wrong action.
- Punishing mistakes. It creates stress and fallout; redirect to the right behavior and reward that.
- Skipping professional help for fear, aggression, or severe anxiety. Those need a qualified trainer, not a chatbot plan.
