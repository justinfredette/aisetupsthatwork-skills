# Will Checklist: Get Your Documents Ready Before You See a Lawyer

## When to use

Adults who keep meaning to “get a will done,” especially parents of minor children. This is prep work, not a DIY will.

## Steps

1. List your assets roughly: property, bank and investment accounts, retirement accounts, life insurance, vehicles, valuables.
2. List debts: mortgage, loans, credit cards.
3. Write down beneficiaries for each major asset and alternates in case.
4. If you have minor children, name your guardianship wishes and talk to those people first.
5. Choose an executor: someone trustworthy who can handle paperwork.
6. Gather existing documents: old wills, powers of attorney, deeds, beneficiary forms.
7. Use the AI to organize all of this into a clean briefing packet for your lawyer.
8. Book the lawyer. The packet makes a one-hour meeting do the work of three.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me organize my estate planning prep before I see a lawyer. You are organizing my information, NOT writing legal documents.

Assets (approximate values are fine):
[property, accounts, investments, retirement, life insurance, vehicles, valuables]

Debts:
[mortgage, loans, credit cards]

Beneficiaries I am considering:
[names and what for]

Minor children: [yes/no, ages]
Guardianship wishes: [names, and whether I have asked them]

Executor candidate: [name]

Existing documents: [old will, trust, POA, divorce decree, prenup: or "none"]

Rules:
- Organize this into sections a lawyer expects: assets, debts, beneficiaries, guardianship, executor, existing documents, questions.
- Flag anything that looks complicated (blended family, business ownership, property in another state) and note "ask the lawyer about this."
- List the documents I should bring to the meeting.
- Give me 10 questions to ask the lawyer in the first meeting.
- Do NOT draft will language, trusts, or powers of attorney. That is the lawyer's job.
- Remind me that beneficiary designations on retirement accounts and insurance override a will.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI may offer to draft will or trust language. Decline. That output is not reviewed, not valid, and potentially harmful.
- Do not paste full account numbers or Social Security numbers into a chat. Names of institutions and approximate values are enough for prep.
- Blended families, businesses, and out-of-state property need a lawyer from the start, not after a DIY attempt.
