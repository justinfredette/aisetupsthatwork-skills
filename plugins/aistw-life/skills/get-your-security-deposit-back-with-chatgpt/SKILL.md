# Get Your Security Deposit Back with ChatGPT

## When to use

For renters who moved out and got back less than expected, or nothing at all, and want to push back in writing before thinking about small claims court.

## Steps

1. Gather your lease, any move-in checklist, your photos, and whatever the landlord sent about the deposit.
2. Upload them with the prompt below. Black out account numbers first.
3. Have the chat use web search to find your state's official tenant rights page. Deposit return deadlines and allowed deductions are set by state law, so go by the official page.
4. Read the deduction review. Common things to question are charges for normal wear and tear and charges without proof, if your state's rules back you up.
5. Ask for a short demand letter that cites the state rule from the official page, lists the deductions you dispute, and gives a date to respond.
6. Send it yourself in a way you can prove, like certified mail or email. If it doesn't work, check your state's small claims court page or free legal aid.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me get my security deposit back. Don't contact my landlord for me.

State and city: [state, city]
Move-out date and deposit amount: [date, amount]
What I got back and when: [amount, date, or nothing yet]
Attached: my lease, the landlord's deduction list (if any), and my move-in and move-out photos.

1. Use web search to find my state's official page on security deposits (attorney general, housing agency, or state tenant handbook). Link it. Tell me the return deadline and what landlords may deduct, quoting the official page.
2. Go through each deduction and say whether my lease, my photos, or the state rules give me grounds to question it. Mark anything you're unsure about.
3. Draft a polite demand letter under 200 words: dates, deposit amount, disputed deductions, the state rule with its link, and a response date.

Rules: Use only official government sources for the law. If you can't find one, say so. Don't invent laws, deadlines, or penalties. This isn't legal advice.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Deposit rules, deadlines, and penalties are different in every state, and some cities add their own. The chat is only as good as the official page it finds.
- Without move-in photos or a checklist, it's hard to show damage was already there.
- This isn't legal advice. For larger amounts or a landlord who won't respond, talk to legal aid or a tenant lawyer.
