# Write an IEP Evaluation Request Letter

## When to use

Parents who believe their child may need special education services. This is not legal advice; timelines vary by state, so verify your state’s rules.

## Steps

1. Write down specific concerns: what your child struggles with, when you noticed, and how it affects schoolwork.
2. Draft the letter with the prompt below. Keep it factual and dated.
3. Send it to the principal and the special education director. Keep a copy and proof you sent it.
4. Watch for the school’s response: they must tell you whether they will evaluate or are refusing, with reasons.
5. If they agree, you will get a consent form. Sign and return it promptly; the evaluation clock generally starts at written consent.
6. Track the timeline. Federal law generally requires the evaluation within 60 days of your consent, unless your state sets a different period.
7. Ask for the evaluation reports a few days before the eligibility meeting so you can read them first.

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Timelines vary by state. Federal law generally sets 60 days from consent, but your state may differ. Verify locally.
- The school is not obligated to test just because you asked. They must respond, but they can refuse with written reasons.
- A doctor’s note or diagnosis does not guarantee an IEP. The school determines educational eligibility, not doctors.
- Do not paste your child’s full educational or medical records into a chatbot. Describe concerns; keep records private.
