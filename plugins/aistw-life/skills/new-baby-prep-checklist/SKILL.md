# New Baby Prep Checklist (The Short List That Actually Matters)

## When to use

Expecting parents, especially first-timers, who want a calm plan instead of a maximalist registry. Also useful for the second baby when you forgot what you actually used.

## Steps

1. Write down your due date, living situation, and budget range for baby gear.
2. List what you already own or can borrow from family.
3. Paste it into the prompt and ask for a trimester-by-trimester plan.
4. Prioritize the short list first: safe sleep space, car seat, diapers, feeding supplies, a few outfits.
5. Handle logistics by week 36: pediatrician chosen, car seat installed and checked, hospital bag packed.
6. Batch-cook freezer meals in the last month. Future you will be grateful.
7. Set up the support plan: who helps in weeks one and two, and what they will actually do.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Build me a new-baby prep plan. Keep it to what actually matters.

Due date: [date, currently week X]
Living situation: [e.g. "2-bed apartment, laundry in building"]
Budget for baby gear: [e.g. "$1,500 total" or "as little as possible"]
Already own / can borrow: [e.g. "crib from sister, stroller"]
Feeding plan: [breastfeeding / formula / undecided]
Support: [e.g. "mom nearby, partner gets 2 weeks off"]

Rules:
- Organize by trimester: what to do now, what can wait.
- Separate "real essentials" from "nice to have" from "skip it."
- Essentials list must include: safe sleep setup, car seat, diapers, wipes, feeding supplies, a few outfits, postpartum supplies for me.
- Logistics section: choose pediatrician by [36 weeks], install car seat and get it checked, pack hospital bag, pre-cook freezer meals.
- Tell me what NOT to buy: the registry traps (wipe warmers, dozens of newborn outfits, fancy bedding).
- Remind me of safe sleep basics: bare crib, firm flat mattress, no pillows or bumpers.
- You plan. I buy, install, and call the pediatrician myself.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI may generate a maximalist registry. Force the three-tier sort: essentials, nice-to-have, skip.
- Car seat advice varies by model. Installation must be checked against your specific seat manual, ideally inspected in person.
- It cannot choose your pediatrician or hospital. It can give you the questions to ask when you call.
