# Write a Rental Listing That Gets Replies

## When to use

Landlords and property managers listing a unit themselves, especially first-timers whose current listing reads like a tax form.

## Steps

1. Gather the facts: rent, deposit, beds, baths, square feet, lease term, move-in date.
2. Decide pet policy, parking, and what utilities are included. Vague policies waste everyone’s time.
3. List the three best features and the three nearest perks: transit, schools, parks, shops.
4. Paste it all into the prompt and get a headline plus full description.
5. Add your screening requirements upfront: income, credit, references. This filters for you.
6. Pair the copy with bright, honest photos of every room. Photos get the click; copy gets the reply.
7. Post it yourself on Zillow, Apartments.com, or Facebook Marketplace. Respond within 24 hours.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Write me a rental listing that gets replies.

Property: [e.g. "2-bed, 1-bath apartment, 950 sq ft, second floor"]
Rent: [e.g. "$1,400/month"]
Deposit: [e.g. "one month"]
Lease: [e.g. "12 months, available Nov 1"]
Pet policy: [e.g. "cats ok, no dogs": be exact]
Parking: [e.g. "one off-street spot"]
Utilities: [what's included, e.g. "heat and hot water included"]
Best features: [e.g. "renovated kitchen, in-unit laundry, big backyard"]
Nearby: [e.g. "10-min walk to train, elementary school 2 blocks"]
Screening: [e.g. "income 3x rent, credit check, references"]

Rules:
- Headline first: beds, standout feature, rent. No clickbait.
- Description: specs up top, then features, then neighborhood, then terms.
- State pet policy, parking, and screening requirements plainly. No surprises later.
- End with a call to action: how to contact me and schedule a viewing.
- Honest adjectives only. If it's "cozy," say the square footage and let readers decide.
- Check for fair housing issues: describe the property, never the "ideal tenant" by protected class.
- Draft only. I post it and handle inquiries myself.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI may write “perfect for a young professional couple.” That is a fair housing red flag. Describe the unit, not the renter.
- Forgetting the call to action. Even a great listing underperforms without “message me to schedule a viewing.”
- Photos matter more than copy. A great description with dark blurry photos still loses to an average one with bright clear shots.
