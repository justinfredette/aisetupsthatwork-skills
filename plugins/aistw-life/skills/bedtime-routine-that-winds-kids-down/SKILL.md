# Bedtime Routine That Actually Winds Kids Down

## When to use

Parents of kids roughly 2 to 12 whose bedtimes stretch into an hour of negotiations, extra drinks of water, and sudden urgent questions.

## Steps

1. Write down each child’s age, current bedtime, and what time they actually fall asleep.
2. Note the top three bedtime problems: stalling, screens, roughhousing, anxiety, or something else.
3. Paste it into the prompt and ask for a 30-minute wind-down plus a 5-step sequence.
4. Print the sequence or put it on the wall where kids can see it.
5. Run it in the same order for two full weeks before judging it. Consistency is the mechanism.
6. After two weeks, tell the AI what still breaks and ask for one targeted fix.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Help me build a bedtime routine that actually winds my kids down.

Kids: [ages, e.g. "4 and 7"]
Target lights-out: [time, e.g. "8:00 PM"]
Current problems: [e.g. "stalling with questions, tablet until bed, second wind at 7:45"]
Wake time needed: [e.g. "6:45 AM for school"]

Rules:
- Total routine 30 minutes max, same 4 to 6 steps in the same order every night.
- Wind-down should get calmer as it goes: active to quiet, never the reverse.
- Screens off at least 60 minutes before lights-out.
- Give me a printable step list plus one sentence I can repeat when they stall.
- Suggest age tweaks if my kids are far apart in age.
- This is a parenting aid, not medical advice. Flag anything that sounds like a sleep disorder and tell me to ask our pediatrician.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI may suggest a routine that is too long to survive a hard night. If it lists eight steps, ask it to cut to five.
- Advice can skew generic for teens. A 13-year-old needs negotiation, not a picture chart.
- If the routine “works” but mornings are still miserable, bedtime is probably too late. Move it earlier by 15 minutes.
