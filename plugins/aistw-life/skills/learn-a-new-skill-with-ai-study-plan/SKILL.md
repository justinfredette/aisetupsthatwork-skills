# Learn a new skill with an AI study plan

## When to use

Anyone with a skill on the someday list and two free evenings a week who has never finished a self-study course.

## Steps

1. Name the skill and your deadline (or "no deadline, steady pace").
2. Ask for the 20% of the topic that covers 80% of real use. Learn that first.
3. Have it build a week-by-week plan using ONLY your real available hours.
4. Each study session: learn one concept, then make the AI quiz you before moving on.
5. Every two weeks, do a tiny project with the skill instead of another lesson.
6. When stuck, ask for an explanation "like I am a complete beginner," then a harder one.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
I want to learn [skill]. I am a complete beginner. I can study [X hours] on [days].

First: what is the 20% of this skill that covers 80% of real-world use? List it.
Then: build me a week-by-week plan using only my available hours. Each week needs: what to learn, one hands-on task, and one short quiz to prove I got it.
Rules: no 10-hour weeks disguised as "intensive." If I only have 4 hours, plan 4 hours. Include one revision week every 4 weeks.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- The AI will happily plan 15-hour weeks unless you state your real hours, then it still might; check the math.
- Reading plans feels like progress and is not. The quizzes and projects are the actual learning.
- It cannot verify your answers against a real exam or standard; use it for practice, not certification.
