# Organize Family Photos (A Folder System You Will Keep Up)

## When to use

Anyone with 10,000 unsorted photos across three phones and a dead laptop. The system matters more than the backlog.

## Steps

1. List every place photos live: phones, computers, cloud accounts, social media, printed boxes.
2. Pick one home base: Google Photos, iCloud, or a folder on an external drive.
3. Ask the AI for a folder system: one master folder, year folders, then month or event subfolders.
4. Start with this month’s photos and work forward. Do not start with 2014.
5. Do one monthly session: move, delete duplicates and screenshots, star the best ten.
6. Back up in two places. Phones break and services close.
7. Once a year, print the best 50 to 100 in a photo book. Prints outlive everything.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Design me a family photo organization system I will actually maintain.

Where my photos live now:
[e.g. "iPhone camera roll, old Android in a drawer, Google Photos, a box of prints, Facebook"]

Home base I want: [e.g. "Google Photos" / "external SSD" / "iCloud"]
Family members contributing: [e.g. "me and my partner's phones"]
Oldest photos to include: [e.g. "back to 2015" or "just going forward"]
Prints to scan: [yes/no, roughly how many]

Rules:
- Folder structure: one master folder, then year folders (2024, 2025), then month folders named "01-January" style or event folders.
- Give me file-naming rules for events: e.g. "2025_Beach_Trip".
- Monthly routine: 20 minutes, exact steps (move, delete, star favorites, back up).
- Tell me how to handle duplicates, screenshots, and blurry shots: delete ruthlessly.
- Backup rule: two copies minimum, one off-site or cloud.
- Start-forward plan: organize this month first, then work backward one month at a time.
- You design the system. I do the sorting.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- Building an elaborate tagging system you abandon in a month. Year and month folders beat a 40-tag taxonomy.
- Trying to organize a decade at once. That project dies. One month forward, then one month back.
- Single-copy storage. A phone in a lake erases years. Two copies, one off-site.
