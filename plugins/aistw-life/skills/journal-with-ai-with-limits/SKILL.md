# Journal With AI and Know Where to Stop

## When to use

People who journal or want to start. AI is not a therapist and not a replacement for professional care; if you are in crisis, call or text 988 (the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) or call 911.

## Steps

1. Decide what journaling is for: reflection and organizing thoughts, not diagnosis or treatment.
2. Set your boundary up front: no crisis topics, no medical decisions, no asking the AI what is wrong with you.
3. Use the prompt below to get reflection questions on a topic, not answers about your mental health.
4. Write your actual reflections in your own words. Let the AI prompt, not opine.
5. Read AI responses critically. AI tends to agree with you and can sound confident while being wrong.
6. Keep your most sensitive entries in a private offline journal. Chat content can be stored or used for training.
7. If your entries keep circling distress, or you feel worse, talk to a licensed mental health professional.

## Prompt

Fill the [BRACKETED] parts with the user, then follow this prompt:

```
Act as a journaling partner for reflection only. You are NOT a therapist and you do not diagnose.

Topic I want to reflect on: [a few sentences]

Rules:
- Ask me 3 to 5 open-ended reflection questions about this topic.
- Do not diagnose, label, or suggest I have any condition.
- Do not give medical or therapeutic advice.
- If I describe thoughts of harming myself or others, stop and tell me to call 911 or to call or text 988 (the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) right away.
- Remind me once that you are an AI, not a professional.

Review the questions before you use them, and skip any that do not feel right.
```

## Guardrails

- Draft first and show the user. Never send, post, pay, book, cancel or delete without the user's explicit approval of that exact action.
- Use only facts the user gives you or that you can cite. If something is missing, ask instead of guessing.

## Common snags

- AI is not a safe or effective replacement for a licensed mental health professional. Do not use it as one.
- AI tends to agree with you. Constant validation can reinforce distorted or unhelpful thoughts instead of challenging them.
- Chat content can be stored, kept, and shared, including sensitive personal information. Keep the most private entries offline.
- Never rely on AI in a crisis. If you have thoughts of harming yourself or others, call 911 or call or text 988 (the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) or go to the nearest emergency department.
